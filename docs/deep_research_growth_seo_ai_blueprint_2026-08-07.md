# Waters & Co — Deep Research, Growth, SEO & AI-Agent Blueprint

Prepared 7 August 2026 · Perth, Western Australia
Scope: business model, portfolio, positioning, organic search, local search, marketing, commercial structure, delivery operations, governance, measurement and responsible AI.

**Decision in one sentence:** Waters & Co should become the practical implementation partner for people and small businesses who need a real problem solved—using AI where it improves the outcome, and patient human support where trust matters more.

## 1. Executive decision

Waters & Co has a strong theme but an unusually broad catalogue: 16 services across business support, AI systems, seniors and families, content, and property. That range is an asset operationally, but it is too broad to market as 16 equal offers. Search engines and customers understand focused expertise more easily than a catalogue of unrelated tasks.

The recommended structure is a portfolio business with four customer-facing practices, supported by one shared operating system:

- **Local Business Growth** — Google Business Profile, reviews and missed-call response.
- **AI Solutions for Small Business** — practical AI setup, workflow automation and managed lead response.
- **Personal Digital Support** — patient technology, digital organisation, pension paperwork navigation, downsizing and memory digitisation.
- **Specialist Projects** — content repurposing, grant matching and valuation-objection preparation.

The first two practices should drive acquisition and recurring revenue. Personal Digital Support should grow mainly through trusted local referrals. Specialist Projects should use highly focused search pages and partnerships rather than broad brand advertising.

**Recommended master promise**

> Practical support and AI solutions for problems that cost you time, money or peace of mind.

This keeps the established human promise and makes AI a capability, not a gimmick. It also avoids implying that every customer problem should be solved with AI.

**The most important commercial change**

Do not ask the market to understand Waters & Co all at once. Lead with the problem the visitor already knows they have, then reveal adjacent services after trust is earned.

## 2. What the evidence says

### 2.1 The addressable market is large, but cautious

Small businesses make up roughly 97–98% of Australian businesses, and Western Australia has well over 200,000 small businesses in the most recently published state data. This supports a substantial local market for practical, affordable support. At the same time, adoption data suggests a gap between curiosity and confident implementation.

Two official measures use different definitions and should not be mixed: the Australian Bureau of Statistics reported that 12% of businesses used AI in 2024–25, with small and micro businesses around 11%; the National AI Centre tracker reported 41% of surveyed SMEs were "adopting" AI in 2025. The difference is useful: many businesses are experimenting, while fewer have embedded AI into normal work. Waters & Co should therefore sell safe implementation and measurable workflow outcomes, not AI awareness alone.

### 2.2 Google's current guidance rewards usefulness, not "AI SEO tricks"

Google says the same foundational SEO practices apply to AI features: create unique, valuable content; make pages crawlable; support text with useful images and video; keep business information accurate; and measure performance in Search Console. Google does not require special AI files, special "GEO" markup, or content rewritten merely to sound machine-readable. Scaled AI pages without added value can breach spam policies.

Implication: Waters & Co should publish fewer, stronger pages based on real customer questions, examples, boundaries, prices and outcomes. AI can accelerate research and drafting, but every page needs human review and a reason to exist.

### 2.3 Price transparency is a competitive advantage

Live Perth examples commonly advertise Google Business Profile management from about $300–$399 per month, compared with Waters & Co at $100–$200 per month. AI automation competitors often use consultation-first pricing and broad transformation claims; public market guides commonly place small projects well above Waters & Co's $990–$4,000 range. This makes Waters & Co accessible, but low prices can also signal inexperience.

The answer is not an immediate blanket price rise. Strengthen the offer with a precise scope, acceptance criteria, a clear handover, one revision round, reporting and visible boundaries. After 3–5 documented wins, review pricing and move the lower end upward.

  - content repurposing and valuation objections

Keep existing URLs working with permanent redirects if slugs change. Each indexable page should have one primary intent and should link to the relevant parent practice, two adjacent services and one useful guide.

### 5.2 What every service page needs

1. Customer problem in plain language.
2. Who it is for—and who it is not for.
3. Outcome and inclusions.
4. Price or a defensible range.
5. Customer-level process in 3–5 steps.
6. Timeframe and dependencies.
7. Boundaries and exclusions.
8. Evidence: sample, anonymised example, case study or checklist.
9. FAQs based on real enquiries.
10. One primary CTA.

Do not publish internal prompts, platform architecture, scripts, scoring logic or proprietary delivery methods. Explain the buyer's journey, not the trade secrets.

### 5.4 Technical and entity SEO

- Add validated Organization and appropriate LocalBusiness structured data; use Service, BreadcrumbList and FAQPage only when page content and Google eligibility rules support them.
- Keep business name, service area, website and brand identity consistent across the site, Google Business Profile and trusted directories.
- Create descriptive titles, unique meta descriptions, canonical URLs, XML sitemap, robots rules and meaningful alt text.
- Compress the malachite artwork for each use; do not make the large background the largest page payload.
- Use fast static content for core service copy; forms and effects must not block crawling.
- Add Open Graph images in the brand system and ensure every page has a specific share title and description.
- Validate mobile Core Web Vitals, structured data and indexation after each release.

### 5.5 Google Business Profile

If Waters & Co serves customers at their locations and does not operate a staffed storefront, use a service-area profile and hide the residential address. Google says the profile should accurately represent the real business and service area; overly broad or virtual-office representations are risky. Build the profile around the most representative primary category rather than trying to list every service as a separate identity.

Actions:

- Select the primary category from the main revenue-driving practice; add only accurate secondary categories.
- Define a realistic Perth service area for in-person work; describe Australia-wide remote delivery on the website rather than stretching the map area.
- Upload real branded work samples, process visuals and location-relevant photographs—never stock imagery presented as work.
- Add services with concise descriptions and "from" prices where supported.
- Ask all eligible customers neutrally for genuine feedback; never gate, incentivise or write reviews for them.
- Reply to every review in a way that protects customer privacy.
- Use UTM-tagged website links so GBP leads can be measured.

### 6.2 Partner network

- Accountants and registered BAS agents: reciprocal, clearly bounded bookkeeping referrals.
- IT repair shops: referrals for patient training and digital organisation after hardware issues are resolved.
- Libraries, community centres and retirement communities: educational sessions without hard selling.
- Removalists, organisers, estate planners and photo bureaus: downsizing, digital legacy and digitisation.
- Web designers, marketing freelancers and telecommunications providers: GBP, reviews, missed calls and AI workflow referrals.
- Buyer's agents, valuers and property professionals: only where conflicts, qualifications and advice boundaries are clear.

Use a written referral policy. Do not imply endorsement by government agencies or professional bodies, and disclose commissions where relevant.

### 6.3 Conversion system

Every campaign should lead to a service-specific landing page, not the homepage. The form should capture:

- service/problem;
- business or household context;
- location or remote preference;
- current tool/process;
- desired outcome;
- deadline;
- consent to be contacted.

Do not collect sensitive detail before it is needed. The confirmation page should state the next step and expected email response time. Create a separate secure intake process after fit is confirmed.

## 7. AI-agent operating model

AI agents should make the solo operator more consistent, not create an unsupervised second business. The recommended model is assistive automation with explicit approval gates.

| Agent/workflow | Input | Output | Human gate | Forbidden autonomy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Demand intelligence | Search Console, ad terms, CRM reasons | Weekly opportunity brief | Owner chooses action | No auto-publishing or spend changes |
| Research and content brief | Approved topic, primary sources | Outline, questions, source pack | Owner approves brief | No fabricated examples or claims |
| Drafting and repurposing | Approved brief or recording | Draft article/posts/clips | Editorial review | No bulk doorway pages |
| Lead triage | Form submission | Service match, risk flags, draft reply | Owner sends/quotes | No regulated advice or eligibility decision |
| Proposal builder | Approved scope and pricing table | Proposal, inclusions, exclusions | Owner confirms scope and price | No binding commitment |
| Delivery coordinator | Signed scope, checklists | Tasks, reminders, status | Owner verifies milestones | No access expansion without permission |
| Quality/compliance | Draft/output and rules | Errors, unsupported claims, privacy flags | Owner resolves flags | No self-approval |
| Reporting and retention | CRM, analytics, delivery data | Client report, renewal/referral prompt | Owner checks | No invented attribution |

### 7.1 Knowledge base

Maintain one controlled source of truth containing:

- approved service descriptions and prices;
- scope/exclusion rules;
- brand voice and visual rules;
- customer-safe process descriptions;
- proposal and document templates;
- compliance and escalation matrix;
- approved case studies and evidence;
- platform credentials stored separately in a proper secrets manager.

Every automation should log the source, action, model/tool version where material, approval, result and correction. Use least-privilege access, separate client workspaces, retention periods and an incident procedure.

### 7.2 Responsible AI rules

Australian government guidance recommends testing and monitoring AI tools, understanding what data they use and store, and checking privacy and cyber security. The OAIC states that the Privacy Act applies to uses of AI involving personal information and warns against entering personal—especially sensitive—information into public tools.

Waters & Co should therefore:

- obtain informed client agreement for material AI use;
- minimise and redact personal data before processing;
- avoid public AI tools for identity documents, financial records, health data, passwords or private family information;
- verify consequential outputs;
- maintain a human contact and correction path;
- test failure cases before launch;
- never represent AI-generated material as an independent professional opinion.

## 8. Compliance and trust guardrails

| Service area | Guardrail |
|---|---|
| Bookkeeping | The TPB says providing BAS services for a fee requires registration. Keep BAS preparation/lodgement, BAS advice and representation outside scope unless performed by a registered provider. Review whether any planned transaction coding crosses the statutory BAS-service definition. |
| Pension/Centrelink | Provide administrative preparation and navigation only. Clients use their own identity-linked accounts unless a formally recognised nominee arrangement applies. Do not advise on asset restructuring, investments or entitlement strategies. |
| Property valuation | Explain that the owner lodges and that statutory deadlines vary. Avoid legal, tax or licensed valuation claims unless appropriately qualified. Verify the exact jurisdiction and notice before quoting. |
| Reviews and ads | No fake reviews, review gating, incentives without compliant disclosure, misleading scarcity, guaranteed rankings or unsupported savings claims. |
| AI and personal data | Privacy-by-design, minimum necessary access, client disclosure, secure storage, review and deletion rules. |
| Senior technology | Never take over passwords, impersonate the client, make financial decisions, move funds or retain identity documents unnecessarily. |
| Crypto literacy | Education about concepts and safety only; no investment recommendations, transaction execution, account access or performance claims. |
| Email/SMS | Use consent, sender identification and a functional unsubscribe process; review the Spam Act and Do Not Call obligations before campaigns. |

Before launch, have an Australian lawyer or qualified compliance adviser review the pension, property, bookkeeping, privacy, referral and automated-communications scopes. This report is business research, not legal, tax or financial advice.

## 9. Measurement model

### 9.1 The minimum data set

Track each enquiry from source to margin:

- first-touch source and campaign;
- landing page and service;
- qualified/unqualified and reason;
- response time;
- quote value and outcome;
- revenue, direct software cost and delivery hours;
- gross contribution per job;
- repeat purchase, retention and referral;
- customer-reported outcome.

### 9.2 Scorecard

| Weekly | Monthly | Quarterly |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiries, qualified leads, response time, proposals, wins, capacity | Search impressions/clicks, GBP actions, conversion by page, revenue by service, delivery hours, churn | Margin by service, customer concentration, pricing review, risk review, content pruning, roadmap |

Do not optimise for traffic alone. The north-star measure is qualified contribution per available owner hour, with customer outcome and complaint rate as guardrails.

## 10. Solo-operator business structure

Because Waters & Co is operated alongside a day job, capacity is a strategic constraint.

- Publish response windows that can be met consistently.
- Cap simultaneous custom builds; use a waitlist instead of rushed delivery.
- Standardise discovery, scope, acceptance tests, handover and support.
- Separate setup fees, recurring management and third-party usage charges.
- Require deposits and signed scopes for project work.
- Define support hours, urgent issues and what constitutes a change request.
- Maintain backup/export procedures so clients are not trapped in proprietary tooling.
- Build a vetted referral bench before demand arrives.

Suggested weekly allocation at launch: 40% delivery, 20% sales/follow-up, 15% proof and content, 15% systems/quality, 10% partner development. Adjust using actual delivery-hour data.

## 11. 90-day execution plan

**Days 1–30 — foundation**
- Confirm ABN/trading details, GST status, insurances, service areas, working hours and substantiable qualifications.
- Finalise privacy, terms, AI-use notice, secure intake and referral policy.
- Reorganise website navigation around the four practices without deleting valid service pages.
- Upgrade the five priority service pages and add About/How We Work.
- Configure Search Console, analytics events, conversion tracking and CRM source fields.
- Complete and verify the Google Business Profile using accurate service-area settings.
- Create three bounded diagnostics: local visibility check, workflow triage and senior tech session checklist.

**Days 31–60 — proof**
- Recruit 3–5 tightly scoped pilot customers across GBP, AI tools and one automation.
- Capture baseline, delivered change, outcome, time and permission for anonymised evidence.
- Publish the first six high-intent guides.
- Start ten researched local-business outreach contacts per week.
- Begin five referral-partner conversations per month.
- Install the lead-triage, proposal and QA agent workflows with manual approval.

**Days 61–90 — controlled acquisition**
- Launch a small Google Search test only for pages already converting.
- Publish two case studies and one original checklist download.
- Introduce Get Found, Never Miss the Job and AI Working Session packages.
- Review prices using delivery hours and software costs.
- Pause or revise any service with weak margin, weak demand or high risk.
- Create the next-quarter content plan from real Search Console and enquiry data.

## 12. Twelve-month sequence

1. Prove local visibility and practical AI offers.
2. Productise the repeated delivery patterns.
3. Expand through referral partners and adjacent recurring services.
4. Specialise the best-performing AI workflow into one or two vertical solutions.
5. Delegate low-risk production or administration with documented QA.
6. Raise prices where evidence, demand and delivery reliability justify it.

Do not build a proprietary platform before repeated client work proves the same problem, inputs and willingness to pay.

## 14. What not to do

- Do not market all services with equal weight.
- Do not create dozens of AI-generated suburb pages.
- Do not claim guaranteed rankings, savings, eligibility or automated accuracy.
- Do not let an agent publish, quote, spend, submit, lodge or advise autonomously.
- Do not collect identity documents through a general website form.
- Do not expose internal prompts, scripts, architecture or scoring methods in marketing.
- Do not use "AI-powered" as the main benefit where the customer cares about a simpler outcome.
- Do not scale paid acquisition until conversion and delivery capacity are measured.
