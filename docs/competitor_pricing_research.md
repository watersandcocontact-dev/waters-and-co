**Note:** Most "local SEO" competitors bundle GBP work into a broader SEO retainer ($1,000-2,500+/mo) rather than selling GBP management alone — a pure GBP-only offer like yours is a narrower, cheaper niche most agencies don't isolate.

### Recommended price point

- **One-off cleanup: keep $150-300.** This undercuts Made 4 Tradies ($497) and sits right at/below WorkspaceIn's low-end tier. Comfortably justifiable as "affordable specialist" without looking low-quality — GBP cleanup is a bounded, checklist-driven task, not a trust-heavy service, so being cheap reads as efficient rather than suspicious.
- **Ongoing $100-200/month is a genuine gap-filler** — sits well below every full-SEO competitor found ($297-2,500+/mo). This is your strongest wedge: you're not competing on "SEO," you're competing on "affordable GBP-only management" which almost nobody sells standalone at this price.
- Consider nudging the top tier to $175-225 once you have 5-10 clients and proven turnaround time, since you're currently priced meaningfully below even the cheapest full competitor.

### Non-price differentiation ideas (gaps observed)

- **Free audit as a lead magnet** — multiple competitors (Made 4 Tradies, SEO Agency for Tradies, Dossis Digital) already do this; you need it too just to be competitive, not differentiating, but its absence would be a real gap.
- **Outcome guarantee** — Dossis Digital guarantees "5 real leads in 90 days or we keep working free"; Search Scope offers "no-result-no-fee" reinstatement. A simple guarantee ("if your profile doesn't improve on our 10-point score within 30 days, next month free") is cheap to offer and differentiates from the many agencies that only sell vague retainers.
- **GBP-only positioning is itself the differentiator** — nearly every competitor bundles GBP into a big SEO retainer costing 5-10x more. Market explicitly as "we only do GBP, so it's fast and cheap" rather than trying to look like a full SEO shop.
- **No lock-in / month-to-month** — Top Rankings advertises "contract-free" as a selling point; match this since larger agencies often use annual contracts.

**Market-wide range:** AU reputation management runs roughly $150-2,500/month, with median SME spend cited around $497/month. There's a clear split between cheap self-serve SaaS tools ($75-450/mo) and full-service managed agencies ($500+/mo).

### Recommended price point

- Given no AU trades-specific direct competitor was found bundling this with GBP/local SEO, and the SaaS floor is ~$75-150/mo (NiceJob, Starworks Starter), a sensible entry point is **$100-150/month for a basic review-generation service** (automated request flow + monitoring), rising to **$200-300/month** for a "managed" tier with review responses and monthly reporting — undercutting Starworks' mid/premium tiers and well below Podium/Birdeye/Adaptify.
- **Don't go below ~$75-100/month** — that's the SaaS-tool floor (NiceJob), and going lower on a trust-and-reputation service risks signaling "automated bot, no real oversight," which is a bad look for something literally about trust.
- Consider a **one-off setup fee ($100-200)** for initial review-request campaign build, review widget install, and platform connections — mirrors NiceJob's $199 setup and Podium's $500 network fee, but cheaper.

### Non-price differentiation ideas (gaps observed)

- **Bundle with GBP service** — none of the competitors found combine GBP optimization + review generation as one package; since reviews directly affect GBP ranking, a combined offer ("GBP + Reviews" bundle) is a real differentiator no single competitor offers together at this price tier.
- **Human-managed responses, not just automation** — most SaaS tools (NiceJob, Birdeye, Podium) are self-serve software; a solo operator personally drafting/approving review responses for trades who don't want to log into another dashboard is a service-vs-software wedge.
- **Trade-specific templates** — generic SaaS tools use generic request templates; industry-specific messaging (tone for a plumber vs. an electrician vs. a landscaper) is cheap to build and not something horizontal SaaS tools bother customizing.
- **No lock-in / cancel anytime** — Podium uses annual contracts; matching NiceJob's "no contracts" model is an easy differentiator against the bigger platforms.

| GoHighLevel-based missed-call-text-back builds (generic AU agency pattern) | Setup $500-1,500 + $300-600/month retainer (covers CRM, SMS costs, support) | [aussiebusinessai.com.au](https://aussiebusinessai.com.au/blog/missed-call-text-back-automation-australia) |
| Podium (as AI receptionist add-on, US SaaS used in AU) | Base ~$399-599 USD/mo, AI agent requires Pro plan + paid add-on on top | [wiserreview.com](https://wiserreview.com/blog/podium-pricing/) |

**Market-wide range:** roughly $99-1,299/month depending on tier, with setup fees from $0 (Hey Jodie) up to $2,500 (Fully Booked). Wide spread — this is a fragmented, immature market in AU with no dominant player yet.

### Recommended price point

- Your current **$300-600 setup is mid-pack** — cheaper than Fully Booked ($2,500) and the higher GHL builds ($500-1,500), comparable to the GHL floor. Fine as-is; could trim entry setup to ~$300-400 to undercut the $500 GHL floor and Fully Booked significantly.
- Your **$150-400/month range brackets Valory's entire ladder** (Intro $149 to Premium $499) and beats Fully Booked (~$500/mo) and Podium (~$600+/mo AUD-equivalent). This is well-positioned — **keep the low tier near $150-180/mo** to compete directly with Valory Intro and Hey Jodie ($99, though that's a bare bones self-serve tool with no management), and market the $250-400 tiers as "managed, not just software" against Valory/Fully Booked.
- Flag: since the platform's own per-call/per-minute usage fee is separate and passed through (per your existing notes), make sure quoted competitor prices above are apples-to-apples — some (Hey Jodie) claim "no per-call charges" which is a real edge worth matching or explicitly contrasting against.

### Non-price differentiation ideas (gaps observed)

- **Transparent, itemized usage costs** — your own notes already flag this; it's a genuine differentiator since Podium's hidden carrier/phone-number fees are a documented pain point (5/mo per number, $500 one-time network fee, add-on pricing for AI). Advertise "no hidden fees" explicitly.
- **Trade-specific script/knowledge base out of the box** — most competitors (Valory, Fully Booked, Hey Jodie) are horizontal AI receptionist platforms, not trade-specialized; pre-built scripts for common trades (quoting rules, emergency call triage, typical FAQs per trade) is a real setup-time and trust advantage.
- **Performance-based/hybrid pricing option** — Never Miss a Call's ~10%-of-value-captured model is unusual and could be offered as an alternative tier for skeptical first-time clients ("pay based on what it recovers") to reduce their risk in trying you over an established player.
- **Job-management integration** — Fully Booked markets integration with ServiceM8/Fergus; if you can offer light integration or manual data hand-off into the client's existing job software, that closes a gap smaller/cheaper competitors may skip.

## 4. Land Tax / Council Rates Valuation Objection Service

**Your current pricing:** Not yet set. Industry-typical: flat fee or contingency % of saving.

### Competitors found

Direct competitors are **thin** — this is a genuinely under-served niche in AU. Most search results returned government process pages (SRO Victoria, Revenue NSW, Valuer-General SA), not private service providers with public pricing. Notable findings:

| Entity | What they offer / charge | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Duo Tax Quantity Surveyors | Independent market valuation/appraisal to support land tax objections — no public fee found | [duotax.com.au](https://duotax.com.au/insights/land-tax-objection/) |
| AAP Valuers (Alliance Australia Property) | Registered-valuer reports for objection evidence — no public fee found | [allianceaustraliaproperty.com.au](https://allianceaustraliaproperty.com.au/blog/how-to-object-to-a-land-tax-valuation-in-australia-step-by-step-guide-3f436) |
| Landmark Valuations | ACT unimproved-value objection guidance — no public fee found | [landmark-valuations.com.au](https://www.landmark-valuations.com.au/blog/act-unimproved-value-rates-objection) |
| Property Tax Specialists Australia | Dispute-lodging guidance; notes valuation report + possible legal/tribunal costs — no public fee found | [propertytaxspecialists.com.au](https://propertytaxspecialists.com.au/dispute-land-tax-return/) |
| Aptum Legal (law firm, ex-ATO/SRO staff) | Objections-to-tax-assessments legal service — no public fee found, positioned as premium/expert legal service | [aptumlegal.com.au](https://aptumlegal.com.au/services/objections-to-tax-assessments/) |

**Key finding: lodging an objection is free (government process)** — what you'd be selling is the expertise/evidence-gathering (comparable sales analysis, valuation report commissioning or preparation, drafting the objection submission), not the lodgement itself. **Every private provider found is fully quote-gated** — literally zero public price points surfaced despite extensive searching. This thin/opaque pricing is itself the strategic signal: either (a) the market is small enough that nobody bothers publishing rate cards, or (b) providers deliberately quote-gate a trust/expertise-sensitive service to price-discriminate by property value. Both suggest you have real room to set a price without being directly undercut, but also that prospective clients have no anchor — you'll need to educate them on value, not just compete on a visible number.

### Recommended price point

- **Flat fee model recommended for a solo new entrant** over pure contingency: contingency requires cash-flow patience (objections take ~90 days per SRO/Valuer-General timelines) and is harder to price confidently with no track record.
- Suggested starting structure: **flat fee $400-800** for a standard residential objection (research + evidence gathering + drafting/lodging the objection), scaling up for commercial/complex properties. This is roughly in line with what an independent valuation report alone tends to cost (implied but not confirmed by sources — flag for direct-quote verification), so bundling the report + the objection work into one flat fee is a reasonable value story.
- **Contingency alternative**: 20-30% of the first year's tax/rate saving is the general industry norm cited in adjacent literature (SA's 10%-in-excess-of-market threshold is the bar for a valid objection, not a fee benchmark) — offer as an option for larger commercial properties where the saving could be substantial, but disclose it clearly since a percentage-of-saving fee on a large property could look expensive in absolute terms.
- **Do not price too low.** This is the one service in this set where being cheap is a red flag, not an asset — clients are trusting you with a formal dispute against a government valuation, evidence quality matters (authorities "give far more weight to qualified, independent valuations than owner opinions"), and a bargain-bin price signals you might not commission a proper valuer or build a defensible case. Price at or near what a credentialed report alone would cost, then frame your fee as "report + full objection service" for roughly the same money.

### Non-price differentiation ideas (gaps observed)

- **Publish a fee at all.** Every competitor found is 100% quote-gated with zero public pricing. Simply publishing a clear flat-fee or contingency structure on your site is a differentiator in a market where prospects currently can't compare anyone.
- **No-win, reduced-fee structure** — since success isn't guaranteed (authorities can reject objections), offering a lower flat fee if unsuccessful (e.g. $150 "research and lodge" fee if it fails, full fee only if the objection succeeds and saves money) reduces the trust barrier for a first-time, no-track-record solo operator.
- **State-based specialization** — objection rules, deadlines (60 days is common but the appeal path differs: VCAT in Victoria, ACAT in ACT, tribunal/court elsewhere) and valuation dates vary by state. Positioning as a specialist in your own state's specific process (rather than a generic "Australia-wide" claim) builds more credibility than the mostly generic multi-state content found from competitors.
- **Free eligibility check** — since there's no fee to check whether an objection has merit (compare assessed value vs. recent comparable sales), offering a free 15-minute "is it worth objecting" assessment before charging anything mirrors the "free audit" pattern that works well in the GBP space and lowers the barrier to a first booking.
