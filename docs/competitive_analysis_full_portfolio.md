## TL;DR

- Across all 18 lines, the single most repeated, verifiable competitor weakness is **poor communication after payment** — slow/lost responses, offshore call centres, broken portals, "can't get anyone on the phone." This is Waters & Co's clearest portfolio-wide brand promise: an Australian-based, responsive, transparent operator with a named human contact and published turnaround times.
- The second cross-cutting weakness is **fee opacity and lock-in** — auto-billing after cancellation, undisclosed cleaning/admin charges, percentage fees, success fees on services that are partly free. Waters & Co should win on no-lock-in, flat, published-up-front pricing.
- Pricing strategy: undercut incumbents by roughly 15-30% on published rate while staying profitable as a low-overhead sole trader, and lead with the exact differentiators competitors are most criticised for (speed, transparency, Australian human contact).

## Key Findings

- Competitor pricing is well-documented but mostly vendor-supplied (agency/marketing "from $X" pages); treat headline prices as marketing claims, not audited averages.
- Verifiable negative reviews are strongest for Airbnb management (MadeComfy, Hometime, Houst), NDIS plan management (My Plan Manager, NDSP), and tradie SEO agencies (Online Marketing Gurus). They are weakest for niche admin services (Age Pension help, land tax objections), where few independent reviews exist and wedges are inferred from adjacent services.
- Several lines are effectively "navigation of a free or low-cost government process" (Age Pension, energy rebates, land tax objections, lost super, grant search). The ethical and marketing wedge is transparency: disclose that the underlying process is free/low-cost, and charge only for time saved and stress removed.

## Business-by-Business Analysis

### 1. GBP / Local SEO / GEO for local trades
**Competitors:** HeyTradie, Tradius, Search Scope, Beets & Co, Waypoint Digital, Made4Tradies, Tradies & Creatives Digital, Online Marketing Gurus (OMG).
**Model & pricing (vendor-supplied):** Monthly retainers. Tradius $297/mo; HeyTradie from $300/mo; Tradies & Creatives from $350/mo; reputable-tier agencies $1,000-2,500/mo (Made4Tradies); Search Scope from $1,999/mo. A low-cost overseas tier (~$400/mo) is widely warned against as spam/link-network risk that can trigger GBP suspension.
**Complaints (verifiable, Trustpilot - OMG):** lock-in contracts + auto-billing after cancellation ("cancellation had been confirmed, but I was invoiced and charged again automatically"); no results/poor ROI despite good communication; agencies holding client GBP/Analytics/website hostage ("They still won't return my website or online ad emails"); "can't get anyone on the phone" after signing.
**Industry corroboration (Waypoint Digital):** if an agency keeps your GBP/Analytics/Search Console "locked away, you're a hostage, not a client."
**Gap/opportunity:** No lock-in, month-to-month; client owns all accounts (GBP/Analytics/Search Console in their name); report on booked jobs/calls, not vanity metrics; add GEO (AI answer-engine optimisation for ChatGPT/Perplexity/Google AI Overviews) which most low-cost competitors don't offer.
**Advertising:** Rank your own site as proof of concept; trade Facebook groups; partnerships with trade suppliers/wholesalers; hipages/ServiceM8 user communities; Google Ads on "[trade] SEO [city]"; referral relationships with bookkeepers/accountants who serve tradies.
**Pricing recommendation:** Flat $249-449/mo, no lock-in — below the reputable-agency tier, above the spam tier, positioned on transparency and Australian ownership.

### 2. Review generation & reputation management for local trades
**Competitors:** Starworks, Adaptify, Workspacein, plus software (Birdeye, GatherUp, Podium) and the tradie SEO agencies above.
**Model & pricing:** Subscriptions. Workspacein from $299/mo; Adaptify from ~$500/mo; a Starworks market analysis cites a median SME spend of $497/mo (range $150-$2,500/mo); software $99-$200/mo. Full-service ORM agencies run $3,000-$20,000/mo (Xero guide notes this is "rarely necessary for small businesses").
**Complaints:** Overlap with SEO agencies — opacity, contracts, and fake-review risk (a breach of Australian Consumer Law that can draw ACCC action). Xero notes most small businesses can DIY at low/no cost using free tools.
**Gap/opportunity:** Ethical, compliant review-generation only (never fake reviews); simple SMS/NFC request system; flat low fee; bundle with the GBP service (#1).
**Advertising:** Bundle with #1; same trade channels; prospect by searching the local map pack for businesses with fewer than ~20 Google reviews.
**Pricing recommendation:** $99-199/mo standalone, or a bundled discount with #1 — undercutting the ~$299-497 cluster.

### 3. AI missed-call / after-hours reception setup
**Competitors:** AI Answering Service (aiansweringservice.io, $48/mo/200 calls), Hey Jodie (from $99/mo), Valory AI (from $149/mo + setup), AnswerMate (from $199/mo), Propeller Voice Agent ($199/mo), FlowWorks, Aivy; human services (OfficeHQ); US players (Smith.ai, Ruby $325-950/mo).
**Model & pricing:** Flat monthly, often plus a setup fee. Overall range $48-1,299/mo; agencies commonly charge $1,000+ setup + $200-500/mo, frequently white-labelling an existing platform.
**Complaints:** Per-minute overages, setup fees, lock-in, and feature add-on charges that "can double the headline price"; US services have timezone/accent issues; agency builds are opaque white-labels.
**Gap/opportunity:** Transparent flat setup fee, natural Australian voice/accent, integration with ServiceM8/simPRO/AroFlo, no per-minute surprises; offer a "setup + handover" option so the client isn't locked to a monthly reseller margin.
**Advertising:** Trade Facebook groups; ServiceM8/simPRO/AroFlo communities; local business chambers; Google Ads "AI receptionist [city]"; bundle with #1/#2.
**Pricing recommendation:** One-off setup $300-600 + thin managed retainer $79-149/mo, or pure setup-and-handover for a flat fee — undercut the $199+ managed cluster.

### 4. Land tax / council rates valuation objection service
**Competitors:** Duo Tax, Property Tax Specialists, Alliance Australia Property and other independent valuers; a fragmented market. Lodging the objection itself is free via state Valuer-General/Revenue offices.
**Model & pricing:** Independent valuation reports cost money; some operate on success/contingency. Little transparent public pricing.
**Complaints (weak evidence):** Whirlpool DIY sentiment that objections "made no difference"/"fruitless." Inferred wedges: opaque success fees, charging for work that often fails, slow resolution.
**Gap/opportunity - quantified:** In NSW, objections must be lodged within 60 days of the notice's issue date (s.35 Valuation of Land Act 1916) and the Valuer General decides within 90 days; per the Valuer-General's annual reporting, of 2,732 objections lodged in 2021-22, only 28.9% were allowed and 55% were disallowed (Colin Biggers & Paisley analysis). Waters & Co's wedge is radical honesty: explain that lodgement is free, quote a flat fee or modest contingency, take only cases with genuine comparable-sales merit, and publish these realistic success odds and the 60-day deadline.
**Advertising:** Google Ads on "land tax objection [state]" (high-intent, low competition); property-investor groups; partnerships with buyers' agents, accountants and SMSF advisers; PropertyChat/Whirlpool forums.
**Pricing recommendation:** Flat fee per objection, or contingency ~20-25% of the first-year saving, disclosed up front — below opaque valuer-led pricing, and only quoted where comparable evidence supports a likely win.

### 5. Small-business bookkeeping (non-BAS)
**Competitors:** Thousands of local/online bookkeepers; Advancr, Scale Suite, AffordBooksTax, Morelli, offshore firms.
**Model & pricing:** Hourly $40-120/hr (freelance $40-80); fixed monthly $249-749 typical (Advancr), micro-business $125-250/mo, small $300-800/mo; per-transaction $1-3. In-house hire is ~$90,000/yr all-in.
**Complaints:** Unpredictable hourly billing; hidden add-ons (payroll/BAS billed separately); offshore timezone/local-law issues.
**Gap/opportunity:** Fixed monthly with scope in writing; Australian; fast month-end close with a published turnaround; make explicit that BAS lodgement is referred to a registered BAS agent (compliance-safe positioning).
**Advertising:** Local Facebook business groups; Xero/MYOB advisor directories; referral partnerships with accountants who want to offload data entry; LinkedIn.
**Pricing recommendation:** Fixed $150-400/mo micro/small tier — at or below the $249-749 cluster's floor.

### 6. Energy & concession/rebate navigation for seniors/low-income
**Competitors:** Comparison sites (Compare the Market, Compare Energy), retailers (AGL), free financial counsellors, Service NSW, Seniors First content. This is largely an information gap, not an established paid-service market.
**Model & pricing:** No established paid model; the risk is that free financial counsellors and Service NSW already do this.
**Complaints:** Confusion — rebates are "not automatic," must be re-applied for annually; means-tested complexity; state-by-state variation (e.g., NSW Seniors Energy Rebate $200/yr; NSW Low Income Household Rebate $285/yr; QLD ~$386/yr).
**Gap/opportunity:** Done-for-you annual rebate application + review; low flat fee; partner with aged-care/community orgs. Must not simply duplicate free services — position on convenience, completeness and annual re-checking.
**Advertising:** Seniors' community centres, Men's Sheds, U3A, retirement villages, Probus/Rotary; local newspapers; GP/pharmacy noticeboards; adult-children referrers on Facebook.
**Pricing recommendation:** Low flat fee ~$49-99/household/year, or bundle with #11 (senior tech) and #16 (downsizing).

### 7. SME grant-finder & rebate-matching
**Competitors:** The federal business.gov.au grants finder (currently lists 597 active programs across every state/territory, free); The Grants Hub (subscription, 14-day free trial); GrantHelper; Service NSW Business Bureau (free); state portals.
**Model & pricing:** Free government tools compete directly; commercial grant consultants charge for writing (see #9); subscription directories exist.
**Complaints:** Overwhelm — hundreds of programs, hard to know which fit; eligibility mismatches; matched-funding surprises.
**Gap/opportunity:** Curated shortlist + eligibility pre-check as a productised flat-fee report; upsell to grant writing (#9). Be transparent that the base search is free at business.gov.au — you sell the curation and time saved.
**Advertising:** LinkedIn (B2B); local business chambers/Business Enterprise Centres; accountant/bookkeeper referral (cross-sell with #5); Facebook small-business groups.
**Pricing recommendation:** Flat $199-499 grant-match report; or a success-linked add-on tied to #9.

### 8. Age Pension / Centrelink claim & appeal assistance
**Competitors:** PensionLink, Age Pension Solutions (Stuart Bell), Claim My Pension, My Age Pension. Services Australia is free but hard to navigate.
**Model & pricing:** My Age Pension charges $165/hr for advocacy, $132/hr (min $396) if the client is found ineligible, plus package pricing. Others use fixed packages.
**Complaints (weak/adjacent evidence):** Thin independent reviews on the named firms. Adjacent claim-help services show a "pay upfront then go silent" risk (e.g., an Avaana Trustpilot review: "stopped responding after payment has been made... they're quick to take it then don't do any work"). Sector sentiment: hidden fees, unreturned contact.
**Gap/opportunity:** Transparent fixed fee; responsive communication; "we deal with Centrelink so you don't wait on hold"; free eligibility check; a refund guarantee if ineligible (clearer than incumbents). Directly counter the "black-hole after payment" fear.
**Advertising:** Financial advisers (retirement referrals), aged-care placement services, retirement villages, seniors' groups, Facebook (adult children), local press.
**Pricing recommendation:** Flat ~$400-700 per claim, disclosed up front — cheaper and clearer than the hourly $132-165 competitors.

### 9. Freelance grant writing for nonprofits
**Competitors:** GrantHelper, Win With Words, independent grant writers.
**Model & pricing:** Flat $700-2,000 per standard application (Win With Words' survey of 10 AU writers found 7 of 10 quoting flat fees of $700-2,000 incl. GST; 2 of 10 hourly at $70-100/hr, estimating 10-20 hours per application); small apps $500-5,000, large projects $20,000+. Commission/success fees are considered unethical by the Grant Professionals Association and best avoided.
**Complaints:** Cost; uncertainty of success; hourly billing overruns.
**Gap/opportunity:** Fixed-fee per application with clear scope; a visible portfolio of wins; fast turnaround; strictly ethical (no commission). Serve the small NFPs priced out of $2,000 writers.
**Advertising:** LinkedIn; nonprofit networks (Institute of Community Directors, Pro Bono Australia, Our Community); Facebook community groups; council community-grant workshops.
**Pricing recommendation:** $500-1,200 per standard application — undercut the $700-2,000 cluster while remaining profitable at low overhead.

### 10. Lost super & TPD/insurance-in-super claim navigation
**Competitors:** ATO "Find My Super" (free via myGov); no-win-no-fee TPD firms Shine, Aussie Injury Lawyers, Withstand, Claimsplus, Claimo, Super Claims Assist.
**Model & pricing:** Lawyers take a contingency from the payout (TPD payouts cited from $40,000 to $2,000,000); lost-super search is free via ATO/myGov.
**Complaints:** Legal fees erode payouts; process complexity; most people don't know they hold cover.
**Gap/opportunity:** Non-legal navigation — locate lost super for free, identify insurance cover, then refer complex TPD claims to no-win-no-fee lawyers (referral-fee model). Position as a free/low-cost first step and be transparent that the ATO search is free.
**Advertising:** Facebook (injury/illness/carer groups, handled sensitively), GP/allied-health noticeboards, financial counsellors, union newsletters; Google Ads "find lost super."
**Pricing recommendation:** Free or low flat fee for the search + referral revenue from partner lawyers; avoid charging vulnerable clients upfront.

### 11. Senior technology concierge
**Competitors:** The Original PC Doctor (seniors service), My Senior IT ($50/30min), Geeks2U, plus free programs (Telstra/Australia Post digital literacy, the government's Be Connected).
**Model & pricing:** My Senior IT charges $50/30min and explicitly contrasts this with "others [who] demand up to $198 for a minimum one-hour booking," with loyalty/referral discounts. In-home is premium.
**Complaints:** High minimum bookings elsewhere; impersonal "fix and leave"; jargon; NDIS/plan-managed billing complexity.
**Gap/opportunity:** Patient, plain-English, teach-don't-just-fix; fair 30-minute pricing; NDIS self/plan-managed friendly; scam-avoidance education (cross-sell #18). Free first phone triage.
**Advertising:** U3A, Men's Sheds, libraries, retirement villages, Probus, senior community centres; local press; adult-children Facebook referrals; NDIS plan-manager referrals.
**Pricing recommendation:** $50/30min or $80-90/hr in-home — at or below My Senior IT, well below the "$198 minimum" incumbents.

### 12. NDIS plan navigation / support coordination (non-clinical) — HELD
**Competitors:** MyIntegra, Plan Partners, NDSP, Paradigm Pathways, plus many local coordinators.
**Model & pricing:** Billed against the participant's NDIS plan at NDIS price-guide caps (fixed by the scheme).
**Complaints (verifiable):** NDSP - a review states "zero human communication... terminated service and NDSP is now ignoring and withholding my NDIS plan from me!!... DO NOT USE THIS FAKE SERVICE"; My Plan Manager (a large competitor) - "routinely lose invoices, or fail to pay them entirely for weeks on end... impossible to reach on the phone... their online portal has been broken for months"; Plan Partners and MyIntegra carry conflict-of-interest concerns because they sell both coordination and plan management, plus staff-reported poor internal communication (Glassdoor). Note: the NDIS Commission's Own Motion Inquiry found 43% of complaints about support coordinators since 1 January 2022 raised integrity concerns, including 108 notifications alleging fraud, neglect, coercion and abuse.
**Gap/opportunity:** Responsive, named single coordinator (not a call centre); genuine independence (don't also plan-manage → no conflict of interest); guaranteed fast callbacks; plain-English plan walkthroughs.
**Advertising:** Local Area Coordinator (LAC) relationships; allied-health referrals; disability Facebook groups; NDIS provider directories; word of mouth in participant communities.
**Pricing recommendation:** Bill at the NDIS price guide (scheme-fixed) — compete on service quality and responsiveness, not price. **Regulatory note: mandatory registration for support coordinators was paused in December 2025.** Still held per DECISIONS.md pending a scope/qualifications conversation.

### 13. NDIS provider compliance & audit-prep admin — HELD
**Competitors:** Provider+, Sky Staff, Canopus Consultants, MD Home Care, LMS Training (LMSTRG certification bundle $1,490), NDISCompliant (templates).
**Model & pricing:** Consultants $2,500-7,000 for registration/audit prep; policy packs $500-5,000+; certification consulting scales higher; verification audits ~$900-1,500, certification audits ~$3,000-12,000+. Template bundles ~$1,490.
**Complaints:** Generic "cookie-cutter" template policies are a leading cause of audit failures (per the NDIS Commission); high consultant fees; one practitioner notes small providers get "quoted $4,400-8,000+ for documents that should be available at template cost."
**Gap/opportunity:** Affordable but tailored (not generic) audit-prep admin for micro-providers; fixed-fee document customisation + mock audit; ongoing "compliance-as-practice" retainer so renewal isn't a crisis.
**Advertising:** NDIS provider Facebook/LinkedIn groups; auditor referral partnerships; NDIS start-up courses; direct outreach to newly registered providers.
**Pricing recommendation:** $1,500-3,500 tailored audit-prep package — below the $2,500-7,000 consultant cluster, above pure templates, positioned on "tailored, not cookie-cutter." Still held per DECISIONS.md.

### 14. Deceased-estate administration & executor support (non-legal) — HELD
**Competitors:** State Trustees, the Public Trustee in each state, Australian Unity Trustees, The Estate Lawyers; law firms for probate.
**Model & pricing:** Trustees charge a percentage "capital commission" on estate value plus fixed fees (e.g., a 2.2% fee applies to certain matrimonial-asset transfers at one Public Trustee); probate lawyers charge ~$800-2,000 fixed for will execution, with grants of probate/administration ranging $2,500-30,000. Trustee fees scale with asset value.
**Complaints:** Percentage fees are expensive on larger estates; impersonal; slow; families overwhelmed by administrative tasks.
**Gap/opportunity:** Flat-fee non-legal admin coordination (paperwork, notifications, asset listing, liaising with lawyers/agents/banks) at a fraction of percentage-based trustee fees; refer legal work out. Position explicitly on "flat fee vs percentage-of-estate."
**Advertising:** Funeral directors (the key referral partner), estate lawyers, financial advisers, aged-care services, seniors' groups; Google Ads "executor help [city]."
**Pricing recommendation:** Flat $60-90/hr or a packaged flat fee — dramatically below percentage-based trustee commissions on mid/large estates. Still held per DECISIONS.md.

### 15. Short-form video/podcast repurposing (B2B & creators)
**Competitors:** On Replay (AU, 48-hour delivery), Solvis Media, ShortVids; AI tools (Choppity, OpusClip, Podsqueeze at $1-2/podcast, Klap); AIIMS Group (Sydney production).
**Model & pricing:** AI tools $1-2/podcast up to $9-39/mo; managed services on subscription or per-clip; Sydney production packages $300-1,500+.
**Complaints:** Pure-AI tools require manual cleanup and produce generic clips; time cost for DIY; managed services can be expensive.
**Gap/opportunity:** Human-curated + AI-assisted (better than pure AI, cheaper than a full agency); a fast published turnaround (beat On Replay's 48-hour promise); niche down to time-poor B2B/professional-services clients.

### 16. Senior downsizing / move-management & estate cleanout
**Competitors:** Care to Move, Home Moving Planners, We Move It (SEQ); removalists; estate-sale operators.
**Model & pricing:** AU pricing is thin/opaque; international benchmarks put senior move managers at ~$60-120/hr, full-service at $3,000-8,000+, estate sales at 25-50% commission, and junk removal at per-item rates.
**Complaints:** Cost; the emotional/sensitive nature of the work; the burden of coordinating multiple vendors.

### 17. Short-term-rental (Airbnb) co-hosting — HELD
**Competitors:** MadeComfy, Hometime, Houst, KozyGuru, GrowHost, Professional Holiday Homes (from 12% incl. GST), plus local co-hosts.
**Model & pricing:** Management commission 12-25% of revenue (~15% budget, 20-22% full-service, 22-25% premium). Separately, Airbnb replaced its split-fee model (3% host, ~14-16.5% guest) with a single host-only fee of 15.5% deducted from host payouts on the full booking subtotal incl. cleaning fees, rolled out for most Australian hosts by 1 December 2025. **Legal note: co-hosts who handle payments outside Airbnb and aren't licensed real-estate agents may be breaking the law; licensed managers can also list on Stayz/Booking.com.**
**Complaints (verifiable):** MadeComfy - payout disputes/non-payment (Trustpilot owner Anastasia Brandon, Newstead: "Every payout I've received has been incorrect (underpaid)... still withholding money owed from a booking over a month ago, despite multiple emails and even a formal Letter of Demand... I have had to lodge a complaint with QCAT and ACCC"), over-promised occupancy, hidden charges + debt collection, and offshore support (guest "Tracey" on Trustpilot: "stranded at 11:30 at night on a public holiday... no way to contact anyone at the company"; a five-year owner on ProductReview: "Customer support is indian offshore- staff talk over the top of you"); Hometime - cleaning-fee overcharging with no transparency, declining communication where owners feel "gaslit," and coercive guest messaging; Houst - fees "not representative of actual costs" and underperformance vs promises.
**Gap/opportunity:** Transparent commission on net payout (not gross), all fees disclosed, a local Australian owner-operator (not offshore support), honest revenue projections, prompt correct payouts, and multi-platform listing. Ensure real-estate licensing/payment-handling compliance from day one.
**Advertising:** Local property-investor groups (PropertyChat), real-estate agents, mortgage brokers, LinkedIn; target owners of a single property underserved by the big managers; Airbnb host Facebook groups.
**Pricing recommendation:** 12-15% commission on net revenue — undercut the 20%+ full-service incumbents as a local, low-overhead operator, and charge on net (not gross) to look demonstrably honest against competitors criticised for the opposite. **Still held per DECISIONS.md — likely needs a real estate licence in most states incl. WA (prosecution precedent in QLD/VIC).**

### 18. Crypto IT/literacy education for over-40s (mechanics, NOT investment advice)
**Competitors:** Cryptoliteracy, AusBlock (Australian Blockchain Association - nationally recognised short course), Blockchain Collective (ASQA-accredited), The Knowledge Academy, Superprof tutors (from $15/hr), free exchange content (CoinSpot/Kraken), bitcoin.com.au education.

**Gap/opportunity:** Strictly education/mechanics (ASIC-safe, explicitly not advice); patient, over-40-friendly delivery; a scam-avoidance focus; in-home/remote 1:1 (cross-sell #11). Emphasise safety and self-custody basics, never "get rich."
**Advertising:** U3A, libraries, community colleges (adult ed), Men's Sheds; Facebook over-40 groups; local press; cross-sell to #11 senior-tech clients; Meetup "crypto-curious" groups.
**Pricing recommendation:** $60-90/hr 1:1 or a group-workshop flat fee — above Superprof's $15/hr hobbyist tutors (justified by safety, curriculum and over-40 specialism), below accredited-course fees.

## Cross-Business Synthesis: Repeated Competitor Weaknesses

1. **Communication black-holes after payment** — verifiable across Airbnb management (offshore support, owners "gaslit," guests "stranded"), NDIS (broken portals, unanswered phones, "zero human communication"), SEO agencies ("can't get anyone on the phone"), and adjacent claim-help ("stopped responding after payment"). → Portfolio brand promise: a named Australian human with a guaranteed response time.
2. **Fee opacity & lock-in** — auto-billing after cancellation (SEO), undisclosed cleaning/admin charges (Airbnb), percentage fees (trustees; Airbnb gross vs net), success fees on partly-free processes. → Flat, published, no-lock-in pricing.
3. **Offshoring / impersonal service** — offshore call centres (MadeComfy), cookie-cutter templates (NDIS audit prep), fix-and-leave tech support. → Local, personal, teach-don't-just-fix.
4. **Selling what's partly free without disclosure** — Age Pension, energy rebates, land tax objections, lost super and grant search all have free government routes. → Radical transparency becomes the differentiator that builds trust.

## Caveats

- Most pricing is vendor-supplied marketing ("from $X"), not audited; treat as indicative.
- Review evidence is strong for Airbnb/NDIS/SEO, weak for niche admin services (Age Pension, land tax) — some gap wedges are inferred from adjacent-service complaints and are flagged as such.
- The claim that "45% of participants have support coordination funding" could not be cleanly verified against a single authoritative source and should be treated as approximate; NDIA quarterly data suggests support coordination is funded for a majority of adult participants but varies sharply by age group.
- Regulatory flags: Airbnb co-hosting (#17) may require real-estate licensing/trust-account compliance; support coordination (#12) had mandatory registration paused in December 2025; crypto education (#18) must remain strictly non-advice (ASIC); review generation (#2) must avoid fake reviews (ACCC/ACL); bookkeeping (#5) must not lodge BAS without registration as a BAS agent; and Age Pension/lost-super services must be transparent that free government routes exist.
