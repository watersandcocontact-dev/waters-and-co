# Crypto IT / Literacy — Service Scope

Same infrastructure and client base as Senior Tech Concierge: same booking
flow, same 40+ demographic (curious or worried about crypto, want to
understand it safely — not trade it). Three formats, same pattern as Tech
Concierge:

- **1:1 sessions** — hands-on, their own device, their own pace
- **Small-group workshops** — 4-8 people, shared cost, more social/less
  intimidating for first-timers
- **Pre-recorded course** — built once, sold repeatedly. Track this one's
  $/hr separately in the hub once live — after the build cost is recovered,
  ongoing $/hr should be very high since delivery cost per sale is near zero

## What's actually taught (mechanics and safety, nothing else)

- Wallet setup — hot wallet (phone/browser app) vs cold wallet (hardware
  device), what each is actually for, how to choose
- Seed phrase safety — what it is, why it's the single point of failure,
  how to store it (and just as importantly, how NOT to — no photos, no
  cloud notes, no sharing it with "support")
- Centralized exchange (CEX) vs decentralized exchange (DEX) — what the
  practical difference is, custody implications of each
- How transactions and gas fees actually work — why a transfer costs what
  it costs, why it can fail, what "confirmations" mean
- Scam avoidance — the actual current scam patterns (fake support DMs,
  approval-drain scams, fake wallet apps, romance/pig-butchering scams,
  too-good-to-be-true yield schemes) — this is arguably the highest-value
  part of the whole service for this client base

## Hard boundary — bake this into every session, every piece of content

**Mechanics and safety only. Never "you should buy X." Never "this is a
good investment." Never any opinion on price, timing, or whether crypto is
a good idea for their money.** This is education about how the tools work,
full stop — not investment advice, and giving investment advice without an
AFSL (Australian Financial Services Licence) is a real regulatory line,
not just a nice-to-have boundary.

**If a client asks for an investment opinion during a session:**
- Don't answer it, even informally, even as a throwaway "well, personally
  I think..."
- Redirect explicitly: "That's outside what I can help with — that's a
  question for a licensed financial adviser, not me." Same referral-partner
  model as the Lost Super/TPD line (see `wave3-unscoped/lost_super/service_scope.md`)
  — worth using the same AFSL-holding referral partner (or a comparable
  one) rather than building a second relationship from scratch.
- Note it in the hub's case detail (`left_for_you_summary` or notes) so
  there's a record the boundary was actually held, not just assumed.

**Why this is a real line, not just caution:** even "general information"
about specific crypto assets can edge into ASIC's definition of financial
product advice depending on how it's framed. Keep all materials
(worksheets, the pre-recorded course script, workshop slides) reviewed
against this boundary before they go out, not just improvised live.
