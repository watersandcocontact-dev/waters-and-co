# Digital Legacy / Account Organiser — Service Scope

Helping a client build a clear, secure record of their online life — what
accounts exist, where they are, and how a trusted person could reach them
if the client couldn't. Delivered as a guided session, reusing the Tech
Concierge client base and booking flow rather than needing a new audience.

**This is a pre-planning/organisation service done *with* a living client.
It is not estate administration** — see the boundary section below, this
distinction is the whole reason this line is safe to build while
Deceased-Estate Admin stays held.

## What's actually delivered

- **Account inventory** — working through the client's accounts with them
  and building a structured list: email, banking, super, utilities,
  subscriptions, social media, cloud photo storage, loyalty programs,
  anything with a recurring charge or sentimental content. Most people
  have far more than they think.
- **Password manager setup** — installing and configuring a reputable
  password manager, showing them how to use it, and migrating accounts
  into it at a pace they're comfortable with
- **Built-in legacy/inactivity features** — switching on the ones the big
  platforms already provide for free and that almost nobody enables:
  Google Inactive Account Manager, Apple Legacy Contact, Facebook
  memorialisation/legacy contact, and the emergency-access feature in
  whichever password manager was chosen
- **Subscription sweep** — flagging forgotten recurring charges found
  during the inventory (a frequent immediate, tangible win that often pays
  for the session itself)
- **Trusted-person handover plan** — a written, plain-English page saying
  where the record lives and how the nominated person reaches it, so the
  plan actually works when it's needed
- **Optional annual refresh** — a shorter yearly session to update the
  inventory, since accounts drift

## Client base and channel reuse

Same seniors already booking Tech Concierge sessions, and increasingly
their adult children (who are often the ones who actually initiate this,
and who pay). Pitch as a natural follow-on during an existing Tech
Concierge visit — a client who's already trusted you with their devices is
a warm lead. Delivery format is identical to Tech Concierge: one in-person
session, same booking flow, same lead form.

Also a natural adjacency to **Downsizing** — a client organising their
physical belongings is in exactly the right frame of mind to organise the
digital equivalent.

## Market evidence (checked 2026-08-01)

- **Digital Care Services Australia** runs a near-identical "Digital
  Legacy Planning" service (structured account list, passwords organised
  securely, digital assets identified, delivered as a guided session) —
  but only across Wollongong/Kiama/Shellharbour in NSW. A proven model
  operating elsewhere in Australia, with nothing equivalent found serving
  Perth.
- **CHOICE** publishes a consumer guide to digital estate planning, and
  AU-specific 2026 guides exist — mainstream awareness of the problem is
  established, which means less market education is needed on a call.
- Reported scale of the problem: people commonly hold **50–100 online
  accounts**, which is the single most useful number to quote to a
  prospect who thinks they only have a handful.

## Boundaries (don't cross these)

These are the equivalent of the Crypto Literacy line's investment-advice
boundary — hold them the same way.

- **Never take custody of the client's credentials.** The client types
  their own passwords, on their own device, into their own vault. You
  guide the process; you do not collect, store, photograph, write down,
  or hold a copy of any password, PIN, or recovery code — not even
  temporarily, not even if the client offers. If a client insists, that's
  a "let's set it up so your nominated person has it, not me" redirect.
- **Never draft or advise on a will, testamentary document, or who should
  inherit anything.** Enabling a platform's own legacy-contact feature is
  a settings change. Deciding what happens to someone's assets is legal
  work. If the conversation moves toward wills, executors, or probate,
  refer to a solicitor and stop.
- **Don't act as the nominated trusted person yourself**, even if asked.
  That role belongs to family or a solicitor — taking it on would convert
  a clean one-session service into an open-ended custodial obligation.
- **If a formal deceased-estate process is already underway** (the client
  has died, an executor is acting, probate is in motion), that is the held
  Deceased-Estate Admin territory, not this. Hand off, don't blur.
- **Don't advise on financial products** found during the inventory
  (super funds, insurance, investment accounts). Recording that an account
  exists is in scope; opinions on whether it's a good account are not.

## What's out of scope

- Ongoing management of the client's accounts or vault after the session
- Recovering access to accounts the client has already been locked out of
  (that's a Tech Concierge troubleshooting job, priced separately)
- Anything involving crypto wallets or seed phrases — that's the Crypto
  Literacy line, which has its own separate boundaries, and seed-phrase
  handling in particular must never be mixed into a general account sweep
