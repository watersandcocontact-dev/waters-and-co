# Lost Super / TPD Navigation — Service Scope (referral model)

**Read the compliance note below before building this into a real offer —
this is the one "low-risk" business in this batch that still touches
financial-services rules, just at the referral layer rather than the advice
layer.**

## The model: identify + refer, don't advise

1. **Lost/unclaimed super search** — help the client actually use the free
   ATO tools (via their own myGov login) to find lost or unclaimed super
   from old jobs. This part is genuinely just navigation help — the ATO's
   own search is free and public, you're helping someone who finds myGov
   confusing actually do it themselves, not accessing their account for
   them (identity-verified login — their own action, same pattern as every
   other "flag where the client must act themselves" service in this
   portfolio).
2. **TPD (Total & Permanent Disability) entitlement flagging** — many
   people have TPD insurance bundled inside their super without realizing
   it. If in conversation it looks like someone might have an unclaimed
   TPD entitlement (e.g. they mention a permanent injury/condition and
   have super from a past job), you flag that as worth checking — **you do
   not assess their claim, estimate a payout, or advise on their prospects.**
   That's financial advice / insurance claims handling, which requires an
   AFSL (Australian Financial Services Licence) or equivalent authorisation
   you don't have.
3. **Refer to a licensed specialist** (a TPD claims lawyer or AFSL-holding
   adviser) for the actual claim work, and receive a referral fee from that
   specialist for a qualified lead.

## Compliance note — verify before operating

- Referral-fee arrangements involving financial products (super, insurance)
  can carry **disclosure obligations** — the client may need to be told a
  referral fee is being paid if the entitlement is claimed. Confirm the
  exact requirement with whichever specialist/firm you partner with before
  taking a single referral fee; don't assume "just a referral" is
  automatically exempt from disclosure rules.
- **Do not** phrase any marketing or conversation as "we help you claim
  your TPD payout" — that reads as offering claims/advice services. Phrase
  it as "we help you find out if you have unclaimed super or an entitlement
  worth checking, and connect you with a specialist" — identification and
  referral, not claims handling.
- Pick your referral partner(s) carefully — a reputable AFSL holder or
  specialist TPD law firm, not a high-pressure claims-farm operator, since
  your reputation is attached to who you refer people to.

## What you'd actually do in a session

- [ ] Ask about past employers/super funds (a simple conversation, not a
      form)
- [ ] Walk them through the ATO's lost-super search on their own myGov
      login (you don't need their credentials — sit with them or talk them
      through it remotely)
- [ ] If a possible TPD entitlement comes up naturally, note it and offer
      the referral — don't fish for medical/disability details beyond what
      they volunteer
- [ ] Warm-introduce to your referral partner; your job ends there
