# Photo & Memory Digitisation Concierge — Service Scope

Sorting, organising and coordinating the digitisation of a household's
boxes of old photos, slides, negatives and home videos. A bolt-on to
**Downsizing** (cleanouts physically unearth this material, every time)
and to **Tech Concierge** (the same senior client base).

**Read this first — you are not a scanning bureau.** Perth already has
several established scanning services and competing with them on
per-photo price is a losing game. What they don't do is the part clients
actually get stuck on: sorting the shoebox, deciding what's worth
keeping, packing it, dealing with the vendor, and getting the result back
in a form the client can actually use. That's the service. The scanning
itself gets outsourced.

## What's actually delivered

- **Triage and sort** — going through the boxes with the client: what's
  worth digitising, what's duplicated, what's already faded past saving,
  what's someone else's family entirely. This is the bulk of the time and
  the bulk of the value.
- **Cataloguing** — labelling batches by rough era/event/person while the
  client is still there to identify faces, which is the one thing that
  becomes impossible later and is the single most common regret in this
  space
- **Vendor coordination** — obtaining quotes, packing and delivering the
  batch to a Perth scanning service, tracking it, collecting it
- **Handback** — organising the returned files into dated, named folders
  on a USB drive *and* a cloud location, and showing the client how to
  actually open and share them (straight Tech Concierge overlap)
- **Optional light touches** — a small "best of" selection for printing or
  a simple slideshow for a milestone birthday/funeral/anniversary

## Client base and channel reuse

Highest-reuse candidate scored so far on physical adjacency: a Downsizing
walkthrough already involves opening every box in the house. When the
photo boxes surface — and they always do — the sorting conversation is
already happening. Quote it there, in the same visit, before the boxes get
taped shut and put in storage (which is what otherwise happens, and is
exactly why the work never gets done).

Adult children are again a strong secondary audience, particularly around
a parent's move into aged care.

## Market evidence (checked 2026-08-01) — and the honest read

Perth scanning providers found: **Pixelfied** (advertises photo scanning
from $0.60, plus free enhancement/dust-and-spot removal), **videotapes2dvd**
(photo/slide scanning and VHS transfer, $100 minimum order), **Camera
House Perth** (custom-quoted, format-dependent), **Pixels Plus** ($4.50
USB transfer, $80 DVD slideshows), plus mail-in national operators like
ScanCorner and Scan My Photo.

**This is a well-served market for the scanning itself** — which is why
this candidate scored only 3/5 on "documented market gap" and 31 overall,
not in the 33 range. The gap being targeted (nobody does the sorting and
logistics) is *inferred from the service descriptions*, not documented
demand. Treat it as an unvalidated assumption until a real client pays for
it. If two or three Downsizing clients decline the upsell, that's your
answer — don't push it further.

Confirm current per-item rates directly with two vendors before quoting
any job; the figures above come from listings and are indicative only.

## Boundaries

- **Never dispose of any photo or physical media without explicit
  client sign-off**, in writing, item-category by item-category. Same rule
  as Downsizing's disposal boundary, and it matters more here — this is
  irreplaceable and emotionally loaded, and a dispute after the fact would
  be unwinnable.
- **Don't assess value** on anything that might be historically or
  commercially significant (professional prints, negatives of note,
  anything old enough to be an artefact). Refer to an actual archivist or
  valuer, exactly as Downsizing does for antiques.
- **Handle originals as if they're unbackupable, because they are.** Never
  post originals; deliver and collect from the vendor in person.
- **If you find material that's clearly private or distressing**, hand the
  box straight back to the client unreviewed. Don't sort it, don't
  mention its contents.

## What's out of scope

- Doing the scanning yourself at volume (that's a capital purchase and a
  price war you'd lose — the whole model is coordination)
- Photo restoration or retouching (vendors already offer this; pass it
  through)
- Any deceased-estate context where ownership of the material is disputed
  or an executor is acting — same hand-off rule as Downsizing
