# Legal Lead-Gen Channels Not Yet Tried — 2026-08-09

Researched per Owner request to find new client channels "we haven't tried,"
legally. Cross-checked against `PROGRESS.md`, `DECISIONS.md`, and
`docs/video-intel/waters-co-implementation-plan.md` first so nothing here
duplicates cold email (117 sent), light Airtasker-style gig-picking, or the
planned LinkedIn organic push. Each entry states the legal basis so nothing
here needs a second compliance pass before trying it.

## 1. Waters & Co's own Google Business Profile

Check first: does W&C have an optimized GBP? If not, this is the most
obviously missing channel — it's the exact product being sold, running it
well on the business's own listing is both a lead channel and a live case
study ("look what we did for our own profile"). $0 cost, zero legal
question (it's the business's own listing).

## 2. Trade supplier noticeboards / trade-desk partnerships

Physical flyers or a business card at trade-supplier trade desks trades
already visit weekly — Bunnings trade desk, plumbing/electrical wholesalers
(Reece, Tradelink, local electrical wholesalers). Fully legal — no data
involved, it's a static ad in a physical location with the operator's
permission. Near-$0 cost, walks straight into the highest-priority
audience (trades and home services) at a moment they're already thinking
about the job.

## 3. Local Chamber of Commerce / business association membership

E.g. a Perth-area Chamber of Commerce. Gives a member directory listing,
networking events, and warm introduction paths — the opposite of cold
email, and the kind of "who do I know" angle a CRM warm-intro tool would
surface if W&C had contacts there already. Legal (standard paid
membership), modest cost, genuinely untried.

## 4. hipages / ServiceSeeking as an inbound *listing*, not gig-picking

W&C already does light Airtasker-style gig-picking (`OddJobs` line) — this
is different: listing **GBP management / AI Tools / Missed-Call
Reception** as sellable services on hipages/ServiceSeeking, the way
"digital marketing for tradies" providers already do. These platforms run
their own consent/lead model — a business receiving an inbound inquiry via
the platform's own system isn't sending a commercial electronic message,
so the Spam Act simply doesn't apply the way it does to cold email.

## 5. Organic participation in local Facebook/community groups

Explicitly **not** the scraped-engager outbound idea flagged and rejected
in `waters-co-implementation-plan.md` (§4, Privacy Act/ToS risk). This is
the opposite: joining Perth trade/small-business Facebook groups as a real
account and answering questions, offering the free Visibility Check when
genuinely relevant — organic participation, not automated targeting or
unsolicited DM outreach. Legal as long as each group's own self-promotion
rules are followed (varies by group — check before posting, not a blanket
rule).

## 6. Referral push to existing/past clients (activate what's already built)

CLAUDE.md and PROGRESS.md confirm the referral/loyalty program exists and
operates day-to-day — worth checking whether it has actually been *asked*
of every current/past client yet, versus just being available. Asking one
happy client for one introduction is $0 cost, fully legal (it's not a
commercial electronic message to a new contact — existing-client
relationship covers it), and typically the highest-converting channel of
all. Cheapest, fastest thing on this list to actually do.

## 7. Content-swap with existing referral partners (not just referral asks)

The land tax referral-partner emails already exist — this extends the
same relationship into content: offering a short co-branded explainer
(e.g. "how a land tax objection works") for a property manager's *own*
client newsletter. Legal basis is clean because the partner sends it to
their own consenting list, not W&C sending cold — same pattern already
used for GBP/reviews via accountants and property managers, just applied
as content rather than only a referral link.

## 8. Local print/online community classifieds

Community News group mastheads (PerthNow's local paper network) and
similar hyper-local classifieds sections. A published ad, not a message
sent to an individual — Spam Act doesn't apply at all (it only covers
commercial electronic *messages*, not public advertisements). Cheap,
geographically precise, genuinely untried channel.

## One flag, not a new channel: SMS to the existing httpSMS ready-list

Session history (`.remember/archive.md`, week of 2026-08-03) shows an
SMS-ready list was prepped (Tom McCloskey, MG&A, JB Concrete, Cantwell, QA
Tilers) alongside the httpSMS infrastructure. **The Spam Act covers SMS
identically to email** — same consent/sender-ID/unsubscribe rules apply.
Before any of that list gets used for outbound (not just replies to
inbound), it needs the same consent check as email — conspicuously
published number, tied to the person's business role, message relevant to
that role. Flagging so it doesn't get sent under the assumption SMS is a
lighter-touch channel than email; legally it isn't.
