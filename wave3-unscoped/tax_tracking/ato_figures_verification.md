## 1. Instant asset write-off / immediate deduction threshold for individuals

**User's claim:** Work-related equipment items $300 or less get an immediate full deduction; above that, depreciated over effective life.

**Finding — CONFIRMED.** This is correct for individual/employee work-related expense claims, and it is a genuinely different rule from the small business instant asset write-off scheme (which has carried thresholds like $20,000 in recent years and applies to small business entities, not to an employee's personal work-related deductions).

Direct quote, ATO "Tradies – be certain about what you can claim" page (an occupation-specific guide covering the employee-tradie scenario): *"If a tool or item of equipment is only used for work and: cost more than $300 – you can claim a deduction for the cost over a number of years (that is, depreciation or the decline in value); cost $300 or less – you can claim an immediate deduction for the whole cost."* The same $300 rule is repeated on the ATO's "Fixed rate method" (WFH) page and the "Mobile phone, mobile internet and other devices" page, so it is applied consistently across individual work-related deduction categories.

Context (not the user's question, but worth flagging so the tool doesn't conflate the two): the *small business* instant asset write-off threshold was $20,000 for FY2025-26; a permanent $20,000 small-business threshold for FY2026-27 has been announced in the 2026-27 Budget but, as of the research date, had not yet passed into law. This only matters if the sole-trader side of the user's business claims depreciating assets through the small business simplified depreciation rules — it does not affect the $300 employee threshold.

**Source:** https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/tradies-be-certain-about-what-you-can-claim (last updated 27 May 2026)
**Research date:** 31 July 2026

## 2. No-receipt caps

**User's claims:**
(a) up to $300 total in work-related expenses claimable without receipts (combined cap across categories)
(b) laundry up to $150 (counts inside the $300, not additional)
(c) small expenses under $10 each up to $200 total per year
(d) phone/internet claims of $50 or less total need no itemised records (above that needs a 4-week representative diary)

**Finding — all four figures CONFIRMED**, with two clarifying nuances worth building into the tool's logic:

- **(a) $300 total — CONFIRMED**, but this is a *substantiation exception* (you don't need receipts, but you still need a record showing you spent the money and how you calculated the claim), not a deduction cap — and it explicitly **excludes car expenses, meal allowances, award transport payments allowance, and travel allowance expenses**, which have their own separate record-keeping rules. Direct quote: *"If your total claim for work-related expenses is $300 or less, you can claim a deduction without full written evidence (receipts)... The $300 deduction limit for work-related expenses does not apply to claims for: car expenses; meal allowances; award transport payments allowance; travel allowance expenses."*
- **(b) Laundry $150 — CONFIRMED exactly**, including that it sits *inside* the $300 rather than being additive: *"This $150 limit for laundry is included in the $300 for total work-related expenses. It does not increase the amount of expenses you can claim without receipts to $450."* The ATO also specifies the calculation method: $1/load (work clothes only) or 50c/load (mixed load).
- **(c) $200 total for items ≤$10 each — CONFIRMED exactly**: *"Small expenses are expenses you incur that are $10 or less... Your total claim for an income year for small expenses must be $200 or less if you record them in this way."* Nuance: this is a **separate provision** from the $300 rule (not a sub-category of it) — it's a relief valve specifically for small, hard-to-get-receipt expenses, and can apply even when total work-related claims exceed $300 overall.
- **(d) Phone/internet $50 — CONFIRMED exactly**: incidental use of $50 or less needs only "basic records to show how you calculated your claim" (based on $0.75/call, $0.10/text); above that, the ATO requires a continuous 4-week representative period of records (itemised bill analysis, or a manually kept diary if the plan isn't itemised), applied across the full income year.

- https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/deductions-you-can-claim/work-related-deductions/tools-computers-and-items-you-use-for-work/mobile-phone-mobile-internet-and-other-devices (last updated 8 June 2026)

## 3. Cents-per-kilometre car expense method

**User's claim:** 91 cents/km for FY2026-27, capped at 5,000 work km per car.

**Finding — CONFIRMED.** The ATO's official rate determination confirms 91 cents/km applies from 1 July 2026 for the 2026-27 income year, and the 5,000km/car cap is unchanged and confirmed on the ATO's main "Cents per kilometre method" guidance page.

Direct quote, ATO Software Developers determination page (status: Current): *"From 1 July 2026, the cents per kilometre rate for calculating motor vehicle expenses is 91 cents per kilometre. This change will apply for the 2026–27 income year. This figure is the base cents per kilometre rate of 89 cents with a temporary one-off uplift of 2 cents per kilometre for the 2026–27 income year. For future income years, the calculated annual indexation rate will be applied to the 2026–27 base cents per kilometre rate of 89 cents."*

For context (not asked, but useful to have baked into the tool's historical table): the main ATO deductions page (last updated with a "Published 4 May 2026" note, i.e., published before the new determination) still shows the historical run: 2024–25 and 2025–26 = 88c, 2023–24 = 85c, 2022–23 = 78c, 2020–21 and 2021–22 = 72c. The 91c figure comes from the newer, dedicated FY2026-27 determination, which is the authoritative source for that specific year. The 5,000km cap ("You can claim a maximum of 5,000 km for work-related use per year") is unchanged and confirmed across years.

## 4. Home office fixed-rate method

**User's claim:** 70c/hour for FY2026-27.

**Finding — CANNOT FULLY CONFIRM for FY2026-27 specifically**, though the figure is well-supported as a reasonable working assumption.

What is solidly confirmed: the ATO's official "Fixed rate method" page (last updated 8 June 2026 — i.e., very recently, close to the research date) states: *"Use the rate for the income year for which you are claiming a deduction: 2024–25 and 2025–26: use 70 cents per work hour; 2022–23 and 2023–24: use 67 cents per work hour; 2020–21 and 2021–22: use 52 cent per work hour."* **This list stops at 2025-26 — the page does not state a rate for 2026-27 as of the research date.**

Unlike the cents-per-km rate (which is set by an annually-updated legislative determination with a built-in indexation formula), the WFH fixed rate is set by the Commissioner via a Practical Compliance Guideline (PCG 2023/1) and is not on a guaranteed annual indexation cycle — it stayed at 70c across both FY2024-25 and FY2025-26 without changing. Third-party tax commentary (accounting firm blogs, calculator sites) generally states or assumes the rate "continues" at 70c for 2026-27, but none of them point to an ATO publication that actually states a 2026-27 figure, and the ATO's own page — updated only weeks before this research — does not contain one.

**Recommendation for the tool:** treat 70c as the FY2026-27 *default/likely* rate (consistent with the pattern of no change since FY2024-25), but flag it in the UI as "unconfirmed for the current year — check ato.gov.au closer to lodgment" rather than presenting it with the same certainty as the 91c cents/km figure, which does have an explicit FY2026-27 legislative source.

**What it covers (confirmed):** electricity/gas, home and mobile internet/data, mobile and home phone usage, and stationery/computer consumables — bundled into the one rate; you cannot claim these separately if using this method. Decline in value of equipment (laptop, monitor, desk, chair etc.) and cleaning of a dedicated home office can still be claimed separately.

**Record-keeping (confirmed, and this has changed from the old methods):** The 4-week "representative diary" shortcut that applied under the old 52c/80c methods **no longer applies** to the current fixed rate method. Since 1 March 2023, the ATO requires a record of **actual hours worked from home for the entire income year** — timesheet, roster, diary, or similar contemporaneous record. Estimates are explicitly not acceptable: *"a record of the number of actual hours you work from home during the entire income year... (an estimate of your hours won't be acceptable)."* You also need at least one record for each category of expense the rate bundles in (e.g., one electricity bill, one phone bill).

**Source:** https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/deductions-you-can-claim/work-related-deductions/working-from-home-expenses/fixed-rate-method (last updated 8 June 2026)

## 5. Concessional superannuation contributions cap FY2026-27

**User's claim:** $32,500 for FY2026-27 (up from $27,500, then $30,000 for FY2024-25). Carry-forward: 5-year window if TSB <$500,000; FY2021-22 unused amounts expire after 30 June 2026.

**Finding — cap CONFIRMED; expiry date CORRECTED.**

The $32,500 cap is confirmed exactly via the ATO's official contributions caps table: *"From 1 July 2026 the general concessional contributions cap is $32,500 as a result of indexation in line with average weekly ordinary time earnings (AWOTE). From 1 July 2024 to 30 June 2026 the general concessional contributions cap was $30,000. From 1 July 2021 to 30 June 2024 the general concessional contributions cap was $27,500."* The user's history of $27,500 → $30,000 → $32,500 is accurate.

Carry-forward eligibility (total super balance under $500,000 at the prior 30 June) is confirmed: *"From 1 July 2018 if you have a total superannuation balance of less than $500,000 on 30 June of the previous financial year, you may be entitled to... make additional concessional contributions for any unused amounts."*

**Correction:** the 5-year carry-forward window is confirmed, but the user's claim that FY2021-22 unused amounts expire after 30 June 2026 is incorrect. The ATO's own worked example establishes the pattern: *"Unused amounts are available for a maximum of 5 years, after which they will expire. For example, a 2019–20 unused cap amount that isn't used by the end of 2024–25 will expire."* Applying the same logic, an FY2021-22 unused amount remains usable through FY2022-23, FY2023-24, FY2024-25, FY2025-26, and **FY2026-27**, expiring only if unused by **30 June 2027** — one full financial year later than the user stated. This means unused FY2021-22 cap space is still available to use *during* the FY2026-27 year the tool is being built for, which is directly relevant to how the tool should present carry-forward eligibility.

**Sources:**
- https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/key-superannuation-rates-and-thresholds/contributions-caps (last updated 24 April 2026)
- https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/super/growing-and-keeping-track-of-your-super/caps-limits-and-tax-on-super-contributions/concessional-contributions-cap

## 6. Non-concessional superannuation contributions cap FY2026-27

**User's claim:** $130,000/year for FY2026-27, bring-forward option up to $390,000 over 3 years.

**Finding — CONFIRMED.** ATO official table: *"2026–27 ... $130,000"* (up from $120,000 in FY2025-26 and FY2024-25). Bring-forward: *"From the 2022–23 financial year if you are under 75 years old... you may be able to make non-concessional contributions of up to 3 times the annual non-concessional contributions cap in a single year."* 3 × $130,000 = $390,000, matching the user's figure exactly. Note the bring-forward amount/period depends on total super balance, and non-concessional cap is nil if your total super balance is at or above the general transfer balance cap ($2 million from 1 July 2025).

## 7. Individual income tax brackets FY2026-27

**User's claim:** Bracket thresholds at $45,000, $135,000, $190,000.

**Finding — CONFIRMED**, with an important addition: the thresholds are unchanged, but the *rate* on the second bracket changes for FY2026-27.

The ATO's dedicated legislation page states: *"As part of the 2025-26 Federal Budget, the government announced from 1 July 2026 they will deliver new tax cuts to every Australian taxpayer... This measure is now law. There are two stages to the tax cuts: From 1 July 2026, the 16 per cent rate will be reduced to 15 per cent. From 1 July 2027, the 15 per cent rate will be reduced further to 14 per cent."*

Confirmed FY2026-27 resident tax brackets (thresholds unchanged from FY2025-26, only the second-bracket rate moves):

| Taxable income | Tax on this income (FY2026-27) |
|---|---|
| $0 – $18,200 | Nil |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 15c for each $1 over $18,200 |
| $45,001 – $135,000 | 30c for each $1 over $45,000 (plus base amount) |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | 37c for each $1 over $135,000 (plus base amount) |
| $190,001+ | 45c for each $1 over $190,000 (plus base amount) |

(Medicare levy of 2% applies on top, separately, per the ATO's tax rates page.) The FY2025-26 table (confirmed directly from the ATO's official rates page) uses the same thresholds but a 16c rate on the second bracket, confirming the 16%→15% change is the one live change for FY2026-27 — no bracket threshold changes are in effect.

**Sources:**
- https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/new-legislation/in-detail/individuals/personal-income-tax-new-tax-cuts-for-every-australian-taxpayer (last updated 13 May 2026 — "This measure is now law")
- https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/tax-rates-australian-residents (last updated 1 June 2026 — official historical/current rate tables, confirms FY2025-26 figures directly)

## 8. GST registration threshold

**User's claim:** $75,000/year turnover (combined across all business activities under one ABN), register within 21 days of crossing it.

**Finding — CONFIRMED, both figures.** ATO official page: *"You must register for GST: when your business or enterprise has a GST turnover (gross income from all businesses minus GST) of $75,000 or more (the GST threshold)..."* and *"Once you are required to register for GST, you need to do so within 21 days"* / *"You need to register within 21 days of your GST turnover exceeding the relevant threshold."* Aggregation across multiple business activities under one ABN is also confirmed: *"You only need to register for GST once, even if you operate more than one business."* (Non-profit threshold is $150,000, not relevant here; taxi/ride-share/limousine operators must register regardless of turnover — also not relevant to a Carpentry TA sole trader, but worth the tool knowing about if scope ever expands.)

This threshold has been stable at $75,000 for a long time and is not indexed annually, so there's no reason to expect it changed for FY2026-27 specifically — no separate FY2026-27 update was found or needed.

**Source:** https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst (last updated 23 May 2025)

## 9. Division 293 tax threshold

**User's claim:** "in the vicinity of $250,000/year" combined income.

**Finding — CONFIRMED as exactly $250,000, not just "in the vicinity."** ATO official table: *"2017–18 onwards ... $250,000"* (up from $300,000 for 2012-13 to 2016-17). This is a flat, unindexed threshold that has not moved since FY2017-18 and there is no indication it will change for FY2026-27. Division 293 tax is an additional 15% tax on the excess over the threshold (or on taxable super contributions, whichever is less), applying when combined Division 293 income + certain super contributions exceed $250,000.

**Source:** https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/key-superannuation-rates-and-thresholds/division-293-tax (last updated 27 August 2025)

## 10. Protective clothing/PPE deduction rules and sun protection

**User's ask:** Confirm sunscreen/sun-protection items are genuinely deductible for outdoor trade work, and confirm general PPE/protective clothing/laundry rules.

**Finding — CONFIRMED**, via the ATO's dedicated tradie occupation guidance (which explicitly covers both the employee-tradie and sole-trader-business scenarios the user described).

Direct quote (employee tradie section): *"You can claim a deduction for protective clothing or items that have features or functions to protect you from specific risks of injury or illness at work. These may include: protective clothing and footwear (for example, hi-vis vests and steel-capped boots); protective and safety equipment as well as occupation health and safety equipment (for example, safety glasses, safety helmets and earmuffs, or sunscreen, sunhats and sunglasses where you are required to work outdoors)."* The small-business section of the same page repeats materially the same list for the sole-trader side of the business.

Additional detail found via secondary sources (worth independently verifying against the ATO's specific "Protective items, equipment and products" page before building strict validation logic, since I did not get to fetch that page's full text directly): sunscreen generally needs to be a TGA-approved product (SPF-rated, AUST L number) to qualify, and mixed work/private use of sunscreen/sunhats needs apportionment. These specific sub-rules are plausible and consistent with general ATO apportionment principles but should be treated as **secondary-source-supported rather than ATO-primary-confirmed** in this research pass — worth a follow-up direct read of https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/deductions-you-can-claim/work-related-deductions/clothes-and-items-you-wear-at-work/protective-items-equipment-and-products before encoding as a hard rule.

Laundry: covered under item 2(b) above — up to $150 without receipts (using $1 or 50c per load), included within (not additional to) the $300 total no-receipt threshold.

**Source:** https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/tradies-be-certain-about-what-you-can-claim (last updated 27 May 2026) — this is effectively the ATO's "carpenter/tradie occupation guide" equivalent; it also links to downloadable PDF occupation guides for "Tradies," "Apprentices," and "Construction workers" specifically, which weren't opened in this pass (PDF links, not fetched) but are the most tightly-scoped official references if more granular carpenter-specific detail is needed later.

## 11. Car/travel deduction rules

**User's claim:** Trips between multiple work sites, or trips carrying bulky tools that can't be securely stored at the workplace, can be deductible; ordinary home-to-single-workplace commuting generally isn't.

**Finding — CONFIRMED, near-verbatim**, via the same ATO tradies guidance page.

Direct quote: *"You may be able to claim the cost of trips between home and work when you transport bulky tools or equipment for work purposes. You can only claim a deduction for the cost of these trips if you meet all of the following conditions: The tools or equipment are essential for your employment duties. The tools or equipment are bulky (this means that because of the size and weight, they are awkward to transport and can only be transported conveniently by the use of a motor vehicle). There is no secure storage area for such items at the workplace location."*

Multi-site travel (as opposed to ordinary single-workplace commuting) is a long-standing, separate basis for deductibility under general ATO travel-expense principles (travel *between* two workplaces, or to alternative/multiple work sites for the same employer in the same day, is treated differently from home-to-usual-workplace commuting) — this general rule is consistent with everything found in this research pass, though this specific research session did not pull the full text of the ATO's dedicated "Trips you can and can't claim" page (linked from the cents-per-km page) to quote it directly. That page — https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/deductions-you-can-claim/work-related-deductions/cars-transport-and-travel/motor-vehicle-and-car-expenses/trips-you-can-and-cant-claim — would be the ideal direct citation for the tool's multi-site-travel logic specifically and is worth a follow-up fetch before hard-coding that half of the rule.

**Source:** https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/tradies-be-certain-about-what-you-can-claim (last updated 27 May 2026) — confirms the bulky-tools rule in full; the multi-site-travel half of the claim is consistent with general ATO principles but wasn't independently re-confirmed against its own dedicated page in this pass.
