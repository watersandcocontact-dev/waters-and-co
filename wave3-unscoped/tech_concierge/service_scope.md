# Senior Tech Concierge — Service Scope & Session Checklist

One-on-one tech help for seniors, in-person or remote (screen share), ~1hr
sessions. Low regulatory risk — no financial, legal, or medical advice
involved, just practical device help.

## Common session types (pick 1-2 focus areas per session, don't try to do everything)

- **Device setup** — new phone/tablet/laptop, account setup, transferring
  photos/contacts from an old device
- **Video calling setup** — FaceTime/WhatsApp/Zoom set up and tested with a
  real call to a family member before you leave
- **Scam & security check** — review recent suspicious texts/emails/calls,
  set up 2FA on email/banking apps, explain common scam patterns (never
  give financial advice — this is "here's what a scam looks like," not
  "here's what to do with your money")
- **Wifi/printer/general troubleshooting** — whatever's actually broken
- **App walkthroughs** — online banking navigation (how the app works, not
  financial advice), online shopping, government service apps (myGov,
  Medicare) navigation only — not filling in forms that require their
  personal decisions

## Session checklist

- [ ] Confirm the 1-2 things they most want help with before arriving (avoid
      scope creep during the session)
- [ ] Bring basic supplies: various charging cables, a notepad for writing
      down passwords/steps in large print for them to keep
- [ ] Test whatever was set up actually works before leaving (e.g. make a
      real video call, not just "it should work")
- [ ] Leave a simple one-page written reminder of what was done / how to
      repeat it themselves
- [ ] Note if anything needs a follow-up session or is beyond scope (e.g. a
      hardware repair, an actual financial/legal decision) — refer out,
      don't attempt it

## What's out of scope (refer out, don't attempt)

- Financial advice or helping them decide what to do with money/investments
- Legal document help (wills, powers of attorney)
- Medical device configuration beyond basic apps (e.g. actual medical alert
  systems should go through their provider)
- Hardware repair beyond basic troubleshooting
