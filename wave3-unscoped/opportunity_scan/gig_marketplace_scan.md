# Gig/Job Marketplace Scanning — take the work, don't always build a business around it

Different from the daily opportunity scan (which looks for new
*recurring* business lines worth building infrastructure for). This is
about picking up **one-off jobs** that match the $0-entry/easy criteria
without necessarily building a dedicated advertised line for each one.

## Where to look

- **Airtasker** — the closest AU equivalent of "things people can't be
  bothered doing themselves," huge range of one-off tasks
- **Facebook Marketplace / local community groups** — "looking for someone
  to..." posts, often lower-competition than dedicated gig platforms
- **Gumtree** — services-wanted section
- **SEEK / Indeed "casual" and "one-off" postings** — less common but worth
  a periodic check for anything matching the skill set already built
- **Upwork/Fiverr** — only for the skills that translate well remotely
  (video repurposing, AI tool setup, crypto literacy content) — not worth
  it for anything physical/local

## The filter (same core criteria, applied fast)

Before taking a one-off job, quick-check:
- Can you actually do it with what you already know or can look up in under
  an hour? (If it needs real new expertise, skip it — that's a rubric
  reject, same as for a recurring business line)
- Does it pay reasonably for the time it'll take? (Rough $/hr sniff test,
  doesn't need the full hub rate-card treatment for a one-off)
- Is it a genuine one-off, or is it actually recurring work in disguise?
  (If a "one-off" task turns into "can you do this every week" — that's a
  signal to consider building it into a proper business line later, log it
  as a candidate in `candidates_log.md`)

## How to log it in the hub — lightweight, not the full treatment

One-off gig work doesn't need its own marketing page, pricing sheet, or
outreach campaign — that would be over-building for a single job. Log it
as a lead under the **OddJobs** business line (added to the hub schema for
exactly this) with the platform it came from noted in the source field.
No content-calendar, no dedicated audit tool, no service scope doc — just
the lead record, same as anything else, so it still feeds the Daily Queue
and the $/hr picture.

## When a "one-off" should graduate into a real business line

If you notice yourself taking the same *kind* of Airtasker/Marketplace job
repeatedly (e.g. three different "help me set up my new phone" tasks in a
month), that's a signal — check it against the full scoring rubric
(`scoring_rubric.md`) and consider whether it deserves the full treatment
(service scope doc, pricing sheet, maybe a website segment) the way Tech
Concierge or Crypto Literacy got. Don't build the infrastructure until the
pattern actually shows up more than once or twice — that's the whole point
of starting with the lightweight OddJobs bucket instead of over-building
for a single job.
