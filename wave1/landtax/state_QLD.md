# QLD — Land Valuation Objection Process

**Researched:** 2026-07-29
**Caveat:** Deadlines, forms, portal URLs, and processing timeframes below were verified against official .gov.au sources (qld.gov.au, qro.qld.gov.au, courts.qld.gov.au, and a department media release) on the research date, but state government processes, forms, and departmental names change periodically (Queensland has a history of renaming the land-valuation department after machinery-of-government changes). Re-verify all details directly against the official sources below before advising any client.

---

## Authority

- **Valuer-General Queensland** — the statutory officer who determines statutory land valuations under the *Land Valuation Act 2010* (Qld).
- Currently sits within the **Department of Natural Resources and Mines, Manufacturing and Regional and Rural Development** (confirmed via a departmental media release dated 11 March 2026 on nrmmrrd.qld.gov.au). This department name has changed with past machinery-of-government reshuffles and should be re-checked at the time of use.
- **Queensland Revenue Office (QRO)** — administers land tax *assessments* (applying the Valuer-General's land value to calculate tax, exemptions, thresholds). QRO explicitly does **not** handle objections to the land value itself — those must go to the Valuer-General.
- **Land Court of Queensland** — hears appeals from Valuer-General objection decisions.

## Deadline

- **60 days** from the date of issue on the valuation notice to lodge an objection with the Valuer-General.
- Example from the 2026 valuation cycle: valuations issued 11 March 2026 (for 15 LGAs, ~560,000 valuations, date of valuation 1 October 2025, effective from 30 June 2026) carried an objection deadline of **Monday 11 May 2026** — consistent with the 60-day rule.
- Late objections may be accepted only if lodged within **1 year** of the valuation notice issue date, and only where the delay was due to the landowner's mental or physical incapacity, an extreme circumstance/extraordinary emergency, or another reason the Valuer-General considers satisfactory.
- Land tax **assessment** objections (with QRO, distinct from land value objections) must generally be lodged within 60 days of the notice date, though QRO states it "may consider a longer timeframe" in some circumstances.

## How to Object

- **Online:** Online Valuation Objection Lodgement System — https://valuations.dnrm.qld.gov.au/objections/ (register, activate via emailed link, then lodge, attach documents, and track/manage the objection).
- **Hard copy forms** (post to PO Box 230, Archerfield BC QLD 4108, or lodge as directed):
  - **Form 58S** — Notice of objection: site value (non-rural land): https://www.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0029/559334/form-58s-site-value-objection-form.pdf
  - **Form 58U** — Notice of objection: unimproved value (rural land): https://www.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0030/559335/form-58u-unimproved-value-objection-form.pdf
- Note: for strata/community title schemes, the valuation is issued to the Body Corporate, and only the Body Corporate — not an individual unit owner — may lodge the objection.
- Land tax **assessment** objections (as opposed to value objections) with QRO: email landtax@treasury.qld.gov.au or post to Queensland Revenue Office, GPO Box 2476, Brisbane QLD 4001.

## Evidence / Grounds Accepted

- The objection must comply with the requirements of the *Land Valuation Act 2010* and provide sufficient information/evidence to demonstrate the valuation is incorrect — typically comparable sales evidence.
- QRO is explicit that it **cannot consider an objection on the grounds that the statutory land value is too high** — that must go to the Valuer-General, not QRO.
- If an objection is incomplete or doesn't meet legal requirements, the department issues a correction notice giving **28 days** to amend and return the objection.
- A dedicated "Reasons for objection" / grounds guidance page exists on qld.gov.au — consult it directly for the full list of acceptable statutory grounds before advising a client (not fully enumerated in the pages fetched during this research).

## What Happens After Lodging

- The department checks the objection meets legal/procedural requirements; if not, issues a correction notice (28 days to fix).
- Objection is assessed by the Valuer-General's office; objector may withdraw the objection at any time before a decision is made.
- Outcome is issued as a written decision.

## Escalation Path

- **Land value objections:** Appeal to the **Land Court of Queensland** within 60 days of the date the Valuer-General's written objection decision is issued. Use **Form 03 — Notice of appeal against the Valuer-General's decision on objection**.
- **Land tax assessment objections (QRO):** After a QRO objection decision, further options are to file a notice of appeal in the **Supreme Court**, or in certain circumstances apply to the **Queensland Civil and Administrative Tribunal (QCAT)** for review — again within 60 days of the objection decision.
