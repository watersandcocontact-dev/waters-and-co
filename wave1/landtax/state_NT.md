# Northern Territory (NT) — Land Valuation Objection Process

*Researched: 2026-07-29. Government processes, forms, and deadlines can change — re-verify all details against official sources before using this for client advice.*

## Authority

- **The Valuer-General** (an independent statutory officer under the *Valuation of Land Act 1963* (NT), sitting within Valuation Services, part of the Department of Lands, Planning and Environment / Department of the Chief Minister and Cabinet family of agencies) determines statutory land values in the NT.
- The value determined is the **Unimproved Capital Value (UCV)** — the vacant/site value of the land, excluding buildings and most improvements. Section 9 of the Act defines UCV as the greater of (a) the capital sum the fee simple might realise if sold with improvements (other than site improvements) assumed not to exist, or (b) improved capital value less the value of improvements (other than site improvements).
- **Land tax:** The Northern Territory does **not** levy a general state/territory land tax — it is the only Australian jurisdiction that doesn't. Confirmed via multiple property-tax reference sources (Duotax, PropertyTaxTools, Odin Tax). This means the UCV produced by the NT Valuer-General is **not** used to calculate a land tax bill. Its primary real-world use is as the basis for **local council general rates** (councils apply their rate-in-the-dollar to the UCV) and it can also be a reference figure for other statutory purposes (e.g. some Crown lease rent reviews). **A service in NT should be scoped and marketed as "council rates valuation objections," not "land tax objections."**
- Contact for Valuation Services: 18–20 Cavenagh Street, Darwin City; postal **GPO Box 1680, Darwin NT 0801**; email **valuation.services@nt.gov.au**.

## Deadline

- **30 days** from the date the notice of valuation is given (the date of posting/issue shown on the notice) — per s18(2) of the *Valuation of Land Act 1963*: an objection must be lodged "within 30 days after notice of the valuation is given."
- The NT.GOV.AU guidance restates this as: object "in writing within 30 days from the date of posting shown on your notice," and instructs owners to check the actual "date to object" printed on the notice (councils/Valuer-General may print the calculated due date directly on the notice, so that printed date should always be treated as authoritative over a manually-calculated 30 days).
- If the Valuer-General's decision on the objection is later disallowed (in full or part), the further review application to NTCAT must also be lodged within **30 days** of that decision (see Escalation Path).
- No general exceptions/extensions were found on official pages searched — flagged in Notes.

## How to Object

1. **Optional first step:** Contact the Valuer-General's office to discuss the concern informally before lodging a formal objection (encouraged by the official guidance; may resolve minor errors, e.g. incorrect property description, without a formal objection).
2. **Lodge the formal objection using the "Objection to Valuation" form** (DOCX, ~259 KB), available at:
   `https://nt.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0010/1567612/objection-to-valuation.DOCX`
   (linked from the official NT.GOV.AU "Statutory valuations" page: `https://nt.gov.au/property/homeowners-and-landlords/understanding-your-property-valuation/statutory-valuations`)
3. The objection must be **in writing**, use the approved form, state the prescribed ground(s) relied on (see below), and be submitted by the deadline date printed on the notice.
4. **A separate form is required for each property** if objecting to more than one UCV valuation.
5. **Lodgement channel:** the process is document-based (complete the DOCX and submit) rather than a self-service online objection portal. Submission would logically be by email (valuation.services@nt.gov.au) or post (GPO Box 1680, Darwin NT 0801) — official pages describe "submit the completed form to the Valuer-General" without spelling out a single mandated channel, so confirm the accepted lodgement method (email vs. post vs. in-person) directly with Valuation Services before advising clients. Flagged in Notes.
6. **NTLIS Valuations Online** (`https://www.ntlis.nt.gov.au/valuations-online`) is a separate, login-gated Land Services Centre portal — from what could be confirmed it appears oriented at viewing/searching valuation and land information records rather than lodging objections; it should not be assumed to be an online objection-lodgement portal without direct confirmation.

## Evidence / Grounds

Under **s19 of the Valuation of Land Act 1963**, an objection must rely on one or more of four **prescribed grounds**:
- (a) that the values determined are too high or too low;
- (b) that the description of the land is not correctly stated;
- (c) that parcels of land that should be included in one valuation have been separately valued; or
- (d) that parcels of land that should be separately valued have been included in the one valuation.

For ground (a) — the ground most relevant to a valuation-reduction service — supporting evidence would typically include comparable sales evidence, valuation methodology critique, and property-specific factors (site constraints, zoning, access, contamination, etc.), consistent with standard practice in other Australian jurisdictions' UCV/site-value objection systems, though the NT sources reviewed did not publish a detailed "acceptable evidence" checklist the way some other states do. This should be verified against the official "Objection to Valuation" form instructions/information sheet before finalising client-facing evidence guidance.

## Escalation Path

**RESOLVED (2026-07-30 follow-up research):** NTCAT is the current, sole statutory review pathway. The apparent conflict between NTCAT (s20A), the "Valuation Board of Review," and the "Land and Valuation Review Tribunal" (Part 7) is explained by a 2025 legislative amendment that restructured this part of the Act — see below.

1. **Valuer-General's decision on the objection (s20):** the Valuer-General must consider the objection and either allow it (in whole or part) or disallow it, and must give written notice of the decision.
2. **Review by NTCAT (s20A), under Part 6 "Review of valuations":** "NTCAT has jurisdiction to review a decision of the Valuer-General under section 20 to disallow an objection or allow it in part only." An application to NTCAT must be lodged within 30 days of the Valuer-General's decision, using NTCAT's Form 1 (Initiating Application): `https://ntcat.nt.gov.au/publications/form-1-initiating-application`, submitted with supporting documents to AGD.ntcat@nt.gov.au. The review is limited to the grounds originally specified in the objection. **This is now the sole current review pathway** (see below).

**What happened to the Valuation Board of Review and the Land and Valuation Review Tribunal:** Directly reading the official consolidated reprint of the *Valuation of Land Act 1963* "as in force at 13 September 2025" (`https://legislation.nt.gov.au/api/sitecore/Act/PDF?id=12255`, extracted via `pdftotext`) confirms both bodies were effectively superseded by the ***Lands, Planning and Environment Legislation Amendment Act 2025* (Act No. 25 of 2025, assented 12 September 2025, commenced 13 September 2025)**:

- The old **"Valuation Board of Review"** was constituted by former **Part VA** of the Act (divs 1–2, former ss20B–20M, inserted in 1987). The 2025 amending Act **repealed Part VA and all of ss20B–20M outright** (endnote citations: "pt VA hdg ... rep No. 25, 2025, s 69"; "s 20B ... rep No. 25, 2025, s 69"; same for ss20C, 20F, 20G, 20H, 20J, 20K, 20L–20M). In its place, **s20A was substituted with new text (No. 25, 2025, s 68)** creating the current NTCAT review right, under a new **Part 6 "Review of valuations"** heading.
- The old **"Land and Valuation Review Tribunal"** (Part 7, ss21–31) was **not repealed as a whole**, but its operative sections **25 and 26 were repealed** by the same 2025 Act ("s 25 ... rep No. 25, 2025, s 72"; "s 26 ... rep No. 25, 2025, s 72") — confirmed directly in the reprint body text, which now jumps straight from s24 (Sittings of the Tribunal) to s27 (Procedure before Tribunal), with no s25/s26 present. What remains of Part 7 (ss21–24, 27–31) is purely administrative/procedural machinery for the Tribunal (its establishment, registries, officers, sittings, procedure, representation, decisions, costs, finality) — **there is no longer any operative provision that refers a valuation objection matter into the Tribunal**. It appears to be a dormant/vestigial body in the current Act, retained in text but with its jurisdictional gateway removed.
- **The Department of Lands, Planning and Environment's `vbr.nt.gov.au` and `environment.nt.gov.au/boards-committees/valuation-board-of-review` pages are stale.** Re-fetched on 2026-07-30 (via text-extraction proxy, as both still 403 direct automated access): `vbr.nt.gov.au` still describes the Board as active, with a 9-member panel and $20 lodgement fee, with **no mention of the 2025 repeal, NTCAT, or any change in status**. **Do not rely on those pages — advise clients that NTCAT (s20A) is the correct and only current review avenue following a Valuer-General objection decision, and that the Valuation Board of Review no longer has legal effect as of 13 September 2025**, notwithstanding what the Department's own web pages still say. This is worth flagging to Valuation Services / the Department as a stale-content issue if the client relationship warrants it.

Beyond NTCAT review, further appeal on a question of law would typically lie to the NT Supreme Court, consistent with general NTCAT appeal structure, but this was not specifically confirmed for valuation matters in the sources reviewed.

## Notes / Unverified Items

The Valuer-General's UCV feeds primarily into **council general rates**. This should be stated explicitly to clients so expectations are correct — this is a rates-valuation-objection service in NT, not a land-tax service.
