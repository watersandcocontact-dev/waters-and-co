# Australian Capital Territory (ACT) — Land Valuation Objection Process

*Researched: 2026-07-29. Government processes, forms, and deadlines can change — re-verify all details against official sources before using this for client advice.*

## Authority

- **Valuation issuer & objections handler:** The **ACT Revenue Office** (Commissioner for ACT Revenue), part of the ACT Chief Minister, Treasury and Economic Development Directorate (CMTEDD), determines and issues **Unimproved Value (UV)** valuations and handles objections to them. Unlike some other states, this is **not** handled by Access Canberra or a separate Valuer-General's office — the ACT Revenue Office employs its own qualified valuers.
- Rates and land tax in the ACT are calculated using the **Average Unimproved Value (AUV)**, which averages the UV over the current year and (typically) the previous four years, to smooth year-to-year volatility.
- Legal basis: the **Rates Act 2004 (ACT)** governs valuations and rates; objection *procedure* (time limits, requirements, decision process) sits under the ACT's standard tax objection framework administered by the ACT Revenue Office (the office cites the same objection process for "assessments or decisions" generally, which explicitly covers land valuation objections).
- Further appeal body: the **ACT Civil and Administrative Tribunal (ACAT)**, acat.act.gov.au.

## Deadline

- **Standard deadline: 60 days.** Per the ACT Revenue Office: *"There is a 60-day time limit to object to a land valuation. This begins when you receive your valuation notice."*
- **Note:** this is measured from the date you **receive your valuation notice**, not from a rates notice date (the office issues an annual valuation notice separately from the rates notice) — confirm which notice the client actually received, as the objection is specifically against the *valuation*, and a separate 60-day limit applies to objecting to a rates *assessment/decision* itself (same 60-day rule, but from the date of that notice).
- **Commercial and rural land exception:** if the owner requests additional information about the valuation **within 28 days** of receiving the valuation notice, the 60-day objection window restarts from the date they receive that additional information.
- **Strict cut-off:** *"We cannot accept valuation objections after the 60-day time limit."* If missed, the owner must wait for the next annual valuation notice to object again.
- **No fee** to lodge an objection with the ACT Revenue Office.
- **Payment obligation continues:** rates/tax must still be paid while an objection is under consideration; if it is not decided within 6 months, interest on any unpaid amount is frozen (an ACT Revenue Office administrative undertaking noted as applying 1 May 2026 – 30 April 2028 — confirm currency before relying on this for a specific client).

## How to Object

1. **Online (preferred, for most residential owners):** via the **"Your property valuation"** service at https://services.revenue.act.gov.au/property/ — sign in with a **myAccount** login to view the valuation, see comparable sales considered (for single residential properties, excluding units), and lodge an objection online.
2. **By post / written submission:** written objection addressed to the **Commissioner for ACT Revenue**, or submitted via the ACT Revenue Office's general contact-us form: https://www.revenue.act.gov.au/help-and-resources/contact-us. Exact postal address should be confirmed on that contact page at time of use.
3. **Units/strata:** if the property is a unit in a residential unit-titled (owners corporation) scheme, the objection **must be lodged by the owners corporation on behalf of all unit owners** — individual unit owners generally cannot object separately.
4. The objection must **"describe in detail why you believe the assessment or decision is incorrect"** and **"include documents that support your case."**

## Evidence / Grounds

The ACT Revenue Office's published guidance is general rather than a prescriptive checklist — it requires the objection to detail the reasons the valuation is wrong and to attach supporting documents. Based on how the office describes its own valuation methodology, the practical grounds that map to that methodology are:
- **Comparable sales evidence** — the office bases UV on sales of vacant land (or improved sales with the value of improvements deducted) within similarity groups (location, use, distance to services); an objection can challenge the sales evidence, groupings, or adjustments used.
- **Incorrect property attributes/characteristics** — e.g. size, location on street, views, development potential, restrictions (power line/sewer easements, contamination), proximity to services — since the office lists these as factors it weighs.
- **Valuation/methodology errors** — e.g. misapplication of the "mass appraisal benchmarking" approach used for standard residential properties, or incorrect application of Crown Lease purpose clause considerations.
- **No explicit statutory list of grounds** was found published in plain-English form on the ACT Revenue Office site; the underlying legal basis for what constitutes a valid objection sits in the Rates Act 2004 and the ACT's general taxation objection provisions — **flagged as unverified in detail below.**

## Escalation Path

1. **Internal objection** to the Commissioner for ACT Revenue (see above) — decided by allowing, partly allowing, or disallowing the objection. **Target timeframe: the ACT Revenue Office "aims to send your objection decision in six months."** (This is a service aim, not a statutory deadline.)
2. **External review at ACAT** if dissatisfied with the objection decision: **must apply within 28 days of the date of the objection decision.**
   - Relevant form appears to be **ACAT Form 24 – Application for Review of a Decision** (found via ACAT's forms library: https://www.acat.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/1361804/af2016-6_application_for_review_of_a_decision.pdf) — confirm this is still the current/correct form at time of use via https://www.acat.act.gov.au/fees-and-forms/forms.
   - **Fees apply** to lodge an ACAT review application (individual and corporate rates differ; concessional rate for not-for-profits or corporations with turnover under $200,000 in the prior financial year). The **exact current dollar amount could not be confirmed** — ACAT's "Summary of Fees" is published as a downloadable PDF/DOC under the *Court Procedures (Fees) Determination*, referenced as being updated effective 1 July 2026; the live figure should be checked directly at https://www.acat.act.gov.au/fees-and-forms/acat-fees before advising a client.
