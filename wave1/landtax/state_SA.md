# South Australia (SA) — Land Valuation Objection Process

*Researched: 2026-07-29. Government processes, forms, and deadlines can change — re-verify all details against official sources before using this for client advice.*

## Authority

- **Office of the Valuer-General (OVG)** is the statutory decision-maker for property valuations (Site Value and Capital Value) in South Australia and the body that receives and determines objections, under the *Valuation of Land Act 1971* (SA). Postal address: GPO Box 1354, Adelaide SA 5001. Objections email: OVGobjections@sa.gov.au. General enquiries: OVGenquiries@sa.gov.au. Phone: (08) 8423 5000 / 1300 653 345 (general), 1300 653 346 (valuations).
- **Land Services SA** is the State's contracted service provider that carries out the day-to-day valuation work — objections received by the OVG are reviewed and assessed by a qualified property valuer from Land Services SA, who makes a recommendation back to the Office of the Valuer-General. The OVG audits that recommendation and makes the final decision (allowing or disallowing the objection). Land Services SA's office (Level 9, 101 Grenfell Street, Adelaide) is also the in-person lodgement point.
- **RevenueSA** administers Land Tax and uses the Site Value set by the Valuer-General; it is not the body that determines valuation objections but is the rating/taxing authority that issues the Land Tax notice which typically starts the clock, and it is the contact for requesting a review of a site value that is "no longer in force" (i.e., objecting to a value for a prior/superseded financial year — see Notes).
- Councils, SA Water, and RevenueSA (Emergency Services Levy) also issue rates/levy notices based on the Capital Value; contact the relevant rating authority (not the OVG) if the dispute is about the **rate in the dollar / amount payable** rather than the underlying valuation.

Source: [Objecting to a Property Value | Office of the Valuer-General](https://valuergeneral.sa.gov.au/valuation/objecting-to-a-valuation)

## Deadline

- **60 days** from the date you receive the **first rates/taxing notice for the financial year** that shows the new valuation — this can be the notice from the Council, SA Water, RevenueSA (Emergency Services Levy), or RevenueSA (Land Tax), whichever you receive first. The OVG's own page states: "your objection must be lodged in writing to the Valuer-General within 60 days of receipt of the first quarter rates notice." The SA.GOV.AU page states the same: "You must lodge your objection within **60 days** of receiving your first rates notice for the financial year."
- **Date of Valuation** each year is **1 January**, and reflects sales/market evidence from the prior calendar year (e.g., 2026‑2027 valuations are based on 2025 sales evidence).
- **No fee** to lodge an initial objection with the Valuer-General.
- **Exceptions / restrictions:**
  - An objection **cannot be lodged against a value that is "no longer in force"** (i.e., a prior financial year's value that has already been superseded by a later valuation). For Land Tax specifically, RevenueSA has a separate "Request review of site value no longer in force" process — see Official Links.
  - Objecting to Site Value and objecting to Capital Value are **treated as separate objections** — "objecting to one value does not necessarily automatically change the other," and a **separate form/application is required for each property and, in the review stages, for each value type**.
  - **"One objection per valuation" rule — RESOLVED 2026-07-30, confirmed from the Act text itself.** Section 24 of the *Valuation of Land Act 1971* was directly retrieved (via a text-extraction proxy against the official legislation.sa.gov.au consolidated PDF, which is normally Cloudflare-blocked to automated fetches — it worked on this pass) and **does** contain exactly this kind of limitation:
    - **s24(1c): "A person may not make an objection to a valuation if the Valuer-General has previously considered an objection by that person to the valuation."** — i.e., one objection per person per valuation, full stop (there is no "unless materially changed" carve-out in the subsection itself).
    - **s24(1b)** provides the relevant exception in practice: if the owner/occupier is later served with a **further notice** of the *same* valuation, they get a fresh right to object to it within 60 days of that further notice — but only if it's the first notice of that valuation served on them under the Act. This is how a **new owner** (who wasn't the person who received/objected to the original notice) gets their own objection right, and it's also the practical mechanism by which someone could theoretically get a second bite if a fresh notice re-issues (rather than a "material change" test as such).
    - **s24(1e):** the Valuer-General "may, for reasonable cause shown by a person entitled to make an objection to a valuation, extend the period within which the objection may be made" — a discretionary deadline-extension power not previously confirmed in this file.
    - Source: *Valuation of Land Act 1971* (SA), consolidated PDF at https://www.legislation.sa.gov.au/_legislation-documents/lz/c/a/valuation-of-land-act-1971/current/1971.113.auth.pdf, ss24(1)–(2).

Source: [Objecting to a Property Value | Office of the Valuer-General](https://valuergeneral.sa.gov.au/valuation/objecting-to-a-valuation); [SA.GOV.AU — Objecting to a property valuation](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/housing/owning-a-property/objecting-to-a-property-valuation)

## Evidence / Grounds

The OVG's guidance does not publish a simple bullet list of statutory "grounds" on its objection page (the page instead points objectors to **Sections 24 and 25 of the *Valuation of Land Act 1971*** for the formal legislative requirements). In practice, based on the OVG's own guidance and corroborating professional/industry sources, an objection should:

- State that the **value is too high or too low** (the most common ground), supported by **comparable sales evidence** from the relevant valuation period (sales in the prior calendar year, since the Date of Valuation is 1 January each year).
- Be a **detailed, evidence-based statement** — the OVG explicitly warns that objections must include a "detailed statement of your grounds for objection," and industry sources note that general, unsupported statements are unlikely to succeed; objections need actual sales/market data, not just an assertion.
- Where relevant, argue that the valuation is **unequal or inconsistent relative to comparable properties** in the same locality/submarket group.
- Where relevant, argue that the **property's physical characteristics or details are incorrect** (e.g., land use code, area, description) — the OVG's site separately addresses "Objecting to Land Use" as a related but distinct process.
- Objections to **Site Value and Capital Value are separate** — choose the correct value (or both) to object to, since a change to one does not automatically flow through to the other.

**RESOLVED 2026-07-30 — verbatim text of s.24 now confirmed from the Act.** Direct access to legislation.sa.gov.au succeeded on this pass via a text-extraction proxy (Cloudflare had blocked it in the original research). Section 24 does **not** actually enumerate a list of specific "grounds" (unlike NT's s19, which lists four prescribed grounds, or presumably other states) — it is procedural rather than substantive on this point. What s24 actually requires (confirmed verbatim):
- **s24(1):** "A person who is dissatisfied with a valuation of land in force under this Act may, by notice in writing served personally or by post on the Valuer-General, object to the valuation."
- **s24(2):** "A notice of objection under subsection (1) must contain a full and detailed statement of the grounds on which the objection is based."
So the earlier characterisation in this file — that SA's Act is open-ended/evidence-based rather than a fixed enumerated list of grounds like NSW's or Victoria's — is now **confirmed correct** directly from the Act text, not just inferred from the OVG's own guidance. There is no separate statutory list of acceptable evidence types beyond this "full and detailed statement" requirement in ss24–25. Source: *Valuation of Land Act 1971* (SA), consolidated PDF, s24.

## Escalation Path

If dissatisfied with the outcome of Step 3 (initial objection decision from the OVG), there are **two mutually exclusive further-review pathways** — you must choose one and cannot run both simultaneously, and choosing one narrows your later options:

1. **Review by Valuer** (s.25B, *Valuation of Land Act 1971*) — an independent, government-appointed Review Valuer reviews the OVG's objection decision.
   - **Application (and fee) must be received within 21 days of receipt of the objection decision.**
   - Fees (2026‑27 financial year): **$136.00** for land used solely as principal place of residence; **$339.00** for any other land.
   - Lodge online, or via printable PDF form; hardcopy to OVGreviews@sa.gov.au, or post to The Registrar – Valuation Review Panel, GPO Box 1354, Adelaide SA 5001, or in person at Land Services SA.
   - A separate application (and fee) is required for each value under review (Site Value and Capital Value are reviewed separately).
   - You nominate your top 3 preferred Review Valuers from a published panel; the OVG approaches them in order of preference.
   - If the Review Valuer determines the value should change by **more than 10%** (increase or decrease), the Valuer-General must alter the valuation roll; if the value is reduced by more than 10%, the application fee is refunded.
   - If still dissatisfied after a Review by Valuer decision, you may then go to **SACAT**.
2. **South Australian Civil and Administrative Tribunal (SACAT)** (s.25C, *Valuation of Land Act 1971*) — can be applied to directly from the original objection decision, or after a Review by Valuer decision (but not both pathways run concurrently).
   - **Application must be lodged with SACAT within 21 days of receipt of the objection decision letter (or the Review by Valuer decision letter, if that path was used first).**
   - Fees (2026‑27 financial year): **$255.00** for an individual applicant; **$319.00** where the applicant is a prescribed corporation.
   - SACAT's decision is final and binding on both parties unless either party seeks **leave to appeal to the Supreme Court** (or requests an internal review by SACAT). You **cannot** use the Review by Valuer process to challenge a SACAT decision.
   - The Valuer-General also has a right to ask SACAT to review the outcome of an independent Review by Valuer.

**Timeframe for the initial objection decision:** the OVG states objection outcomes are "usually provided within **12 weeks**" of receipt of the objection (longer for complex properties). Note: rates and taxes remain due and payable by their due date even while an objection is pending.

**Separately, for RevenueSA-administered tax disputes** (i.e., objecting to an assessment/decision made by RevenueSA itself, as distinct from the underlying Site/Capital Value), RevenueSA's own objections-and-appeals process can lead to appeal at the **Supreme Court of South Australia** (RevenueSA publishes Land Tax appeal outcomes heard by the Supreme Court since 2007) — this is a different track from valuation objections to the OVG and is relevant if the dispute is with RevenueSA's tax assessment/administration rather than the underlying land value.
