# Comparable Sales Evidence — Research Checklist

Comparable sales ("comps") evidence is the strongest ground for a valuation
objection. This checklist covers how to pull it per state, prioritizing free/
public sources first.

## What makes a good comparable

- [ ] Sold reasonably close to the valuation date used by the authority (check
      each state's valuation date — often 1 January or 1 July of a given year,
      NOT the date you're preparing the objection)
- [ ] Similar land size (within ~10-20%)
- [ ] Similar zoning/permitted use
- [ ] Similar location (same suburb/street ideally, same precinct at minimum)
- [ ] Similar improvements if valuing capital improved value (not just land)
- [ ] Arm's-length sale (not a related-party transfer, deceased estate fire
      sale, or distressed/mortgagee sale — these skew low and are often
      excluded/challenged)
- [ ] 3-5 comps minimum for a credible case; more for a stronger case

## Free-ish aggregator sources (cross-check, not authoritative for objections)

- realestate.com.au / domain.com.au sold-price history (often free for basic
  lookups, useful for triangulating, but not always accepted as primary
  evidence by the authority — check each state's accepted evidence rules)
- CoreLogic / RP Data (paid, but often the "gold standard" comps source
  valuers themselves use — worth the cost for higher-value disputes)
- Local real estate agents — often willing to pull recent comparable sales for
  free in exchange for the relationship/future listing goodwill

## Process

1. Identify subject property's valuation date (not today's date).
2. Search 3-5 sales within ~6-12 months of that date, same suburb/precinct.
3. Build a comps table: address | sale date | sale price | land size | key
   differences | adjusted comparison value.
4. Photograph/document any condition differences that justify adjustments.
5. Attach to the objection letter (`objection_letter_template.md`).
