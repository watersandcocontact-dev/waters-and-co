# Land Tax / Rates Objection — Pricing Sheet (draft, adjustable)

**Service area: Australia-wide** — see `objection_letter_template.md`.

**Sourced from competitor research** (`docs/competitor_pricing_research.md`,
2026-07-30). Important context that shapes everything below: **every
private provider found in this space is fully quote-gated — zero public
pricing exists anywhere.** That's not a research gap, it's the finding
itself. Two implications: (1) you have real room to set a price without
being directly undercut by a visible competitor number, and (2) prospects
have no anchor, so you'll need to sell on value/evidence quality, not price
comparison.

## Recommended: flat fee (not contingency, for a new solo operator)

| Property type | Flat fee |
|---|---|
| Standard residential objection | **$400-800** |
| Commercial / complex property | Quote on assessment — scales with evidence-gathering complexity |

Covers: eligibility assessment, comparable-sales evidence research, letter
drafting (see `objection_letter_template.md`), and guidance through
lodgement (client lodges themselves — see the template's notes on
identity-verified portals/signatures).

**Why flat fee over contingency to start:** objections take ~60-90 days to
resolve (see the relevant `state_*.md` doc), so contingency income is slow
and unpredictable — hard to manage cash flow on as a new solo operator with
no track record yet. Revisit contingency once you have a track record.

## Optional alternative: contingency (for larger/commercial properties)

**20-30% of the first year's tax/rate saving.** Disclose this clearly up
front — a percentage fee on a large commercial saving can look expensive in
absolute dollars even though the client is still ahead. Better suited to
commercial clients with bigger, harder-to-self-assess savings than to
straightforward residential cases.

## Do not underprice this one

Land tax/rates objection is the one service in the whole portfolio where
being cheap is a red flag, not an asset. Clients are trusting you with a
formal dispute against a government valuation — evidence quality is what
authorities actually weigh, and a bargain-bin price signals a rushed,
low-quality case. Price at or near what a credentialed valuation report
alone tends to cost, then frame your fee as "report-quality evidence + the
full objection service" for similar money.

## Non-price ways to win the first few clients (no track record yet)

- **Free 15-minute eligibility check** — costs you nothing to assess whether
  an objection has merit (compare assessed value vs. recent comps) before
  charging anything; lowers the barrier to a first booking, same pattern
  as the GBP free-audit offer.
- **No-win, reduced-fee structure** — e.g. $150 "research and lodge" fee if
  the objection is unsuccessful, full fee only if it succeeds. Reduces the
  trust barrier for a first-time client with no reviews/history to check.
- **Publish a price at all** — literally no competitor found does this. Just
  having a visible fee on your site/pitch is itself a differentiator in a
  market where prospects currently can't compare anyone.
- **State-based specialization** — deadlines and escalation paths differ by
  state (NT is 30 days, others 60; VCAT vs ACAT vs Land Court vs SAT, see
  the `state_*.md` docs) — positioning as a specialist in the client's
  specific state's process reads as more credible than a generic
  "Australia-wide" claim, even though you do cover all 8.
