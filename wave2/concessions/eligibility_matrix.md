# Australian State/Territory Concession & Rebate Eligibility Matrix

**Research date:** 29 July 2026
**Purpose:** Background research for a planned "energy & government concession/rebate navigation" service. This document is intended to help identify which programs exist, roughly who qualifies, and where to apply — so a navigator can point a client to the right door.

## Important disclaimers — read before using this with any client

- **This is informational/navigation content only. It is not financial advice, legal advice, or a substitute for professional advice.** No claims or applications should be lodged based on this document alone.
- **These programs change annually.** Dollar amounts are typically indexed or reset each financial year (1 July), and program names, eligibility rules, and application processes are regularly revised in state budgets. Everything below reflects what was publicly findable via web search on the research date above and **must be re-verified against the current official source immediately before use with any real client.**
- Several figures in this document come from search-engine snippets or third-party aggregator sites rather than a confirmed live fetch of the primary .gov.au page (a number of official pages blocked automated fetching during this research — see per-state notes). Where a figure could not be confirmed directly on an official page, or where sources conflicted, it is explicitly **flagged for verification** rather than presented as certain.
- Concession card names and issuers referenced throughout: Pensioner Concession Card (PCC), Health Care Card (HCC), Low Income Health Care Card, Commonwealth Seniors Health Card (CSHC — note this card is *excluded* from several programs below), and DVA (Department of Veterans' Affairs) Gold/White Cards. Always check the specific card list for each program — they are not interchangeable.
- **The federal universal energy bill credit (the "Energy Bill Relief Fund" $75/quarter automatic credit applied to all households) ended 31 December 2025.** As of the research date, no confirmed universal federal top-up exists for 2026. Do not assume clients still receive this — only the state-based, eligibility-tested concessions below remain confirmed as current. Re-check energy.gov.au for any newly announced federal measures before advising clients.
- All energy retailers operating under the National Energy Customer Framework are legally required (via AER-approved hardship policies) to offer payment plans and hardship assistance to residential customers in payment difficulty, regardless of concession card status. This is a standing right, not a discretionary program, and is worth mentioning to every client regardless of state.

## New South Wales (NSW)

| Program | Eligibility | Value | How to Apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low Income Household Rebate** (energy) | NSW resident, electricity account holder (bill in your name), and hold a PCC (Centrelink or DVA), HCC, Low Income HCC, or DVA Veteran Gold Card. CSHC holders are **not** eligible. | **$285/year** for retail (on-market) customers. For embedded network/on-supply customers (e.g. retirement villages, some strata/caravan parks), sources conflict — one figure seen was **$313.50/year**, another **$385/year**. **Flagged: confirm exact on-supply figure on energy.nsw.gov.au or via Service NSW before quoting to a client.** | Retail customers: call your electricity retailer and ask for the rebate to be applied, providing concession card details. On-supply/embedded network customers: apply online via Service NSW or in person at a Service NSW Centre (paper form also available, posted to NSW Energy Rebates, PO Box 435, Parramatta NSW 2124). |
| **Pensioner Council Rate Concession** | Must hold a PCC, a DVA Gold Card embossed "TPI" or "EDA", or be a war widow/widower / wholly dependent partner receiving the DVA income support supplement. Applies to the pensioner's principal place of residence. | Up to **$250/year** off ordinary rates and domestic waste charges, plus up to **$87.50/year** off water rates and **$87.50/year** off sewerage rates (where council provides these services) — so up to ~$425/year combined where all three apply. Cost is shared 55% NSW Government / 45% council. | Apply directly with your local council (not automatic) — complete a council application form and authorise the council to verify eligibility with Centrelink/DVA. |
| **Seniors Card (NSW)** | NSW residents 60+ (Seniors Card) — a separate Senior Savers Card exists for those 60+ still working 20+ hrs/week, with fewer transport benefits. | Not a direct cash rebate — provides discounts from participating businesses plus discounted/free public transport (Gold Opal-style concessions for eligible cardholders). Value varies by usage; no fixed dollar figure to quote. | Apply via Service NSW (online or in person). |
| **Savings Finder / Cost of Living service** | All NSW residents — a free personalised tool. | Not a payment itself — it's a government tool that scans a household's situation and lists every rebate/concession they may be entitled to (energy, health, transport, education, etc.). Useful as a first-stop navigation aid. | service.nsw.gov.au "Savings Finder" (online, or via Service NSW centre appointment). |

**NSW verification note:** the on-supply rebate figure conflict above should be resolved against energy.nsw.gov.au directly before this is used with a client. Application closing dates (seen referenced as 30 June each year) should also be reconfirmed annually.

## Victoria (VIC)

| Program | Eligibility | Value | How to Apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Annual Electricity Concession** | Hold a valid PCC, HCC, or DVA Gold Card. | 17.5% off electricity usage and supply charges for the year, worth approximately **$230.65/year (2025-26 rate)**, applied as a daily bill credit (~63c/day). **Flagged: reconfirm the 2026-27 rate, as this is indexed annually.** | Provide concession card details to your electricity retailer; retailer applies the discount to the bill. |
| **Utility Relief Grant Scheme (URGS)** | Concession card holder (PCC/HCC/DVA Gold Card) experiencing financial hardship due to a specific event (job loss, illness, family violence, natural disaster, unexpected circumstance) with an overdue bill. | Up to **$650 per utility type** (electricity, gas, water) in a rolling 2-year period; up to $1,300 over 2 years if only one utility type (e.g. electricity only) is used. Not guaranteed — amount assessed case by case. | Phone your electricity/gas/water retailer to request an application, or call the Concessions Information Line 1800 658 521. |
| **Municipal (Council) Rates Concession** | Ratepayer who lives at the property as principal residence and holds a valid concession card (PCC, HCC, or DVA Gold Card — **not** just an unemployment/JobSeeker card). | 50% off council rates, capped at **$266.00 (2025-26)**, rising to a cap of **$272.70 for 2026-27**. Some councils (e.g. City of Melbourne, Knox) add their own top-up rebate on top of the state concession. | Contact your local council directly for an application form; not automatic. |
| **Fire Services Property Levy concession** | Automatic for anyone already receiving the Municipal Rates Concession. | **$50 off** the Fire Services Property Levy assessment. | Automatic once the municipal rates concession is granted — no separate application. |
| **Seniors Card (VIC)** | Victorian residents 60+ working under 35 hrs/week. | Discounts from participating businesses; half-price public transport fares (V/Line and metropolitan) for cardholders, plus Victorian public transport concession fare structure. | Apply via Services Victoria (online). |

**VIC verification note:** the $230.65 electricity concession figure and the $266/$272.70 rates caps are both indexed annually — reconfirm current-year figures each July via services.dffh.vic.gov.au before advising a client.

## Queensland (QLD)

| Program | Eligibility | Value | How to Apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Electricity Rebate** | Holders of: Queensland Seniors Card, PCC (Centrelink or DVA), HCC, DVA Veteran Gold Card, or asylum-seeker ImmiCard. Must be the account holder at their principal residence. Foster Child HCC and CSHC holders are **not** eligible. Notably, QLD is one of the few states where the **Seniors Card alone** (not just a pension card) unlocks the electricity rebate. | **$399.47/year (GST inclusive)**, per qld.gov.au directly. (An older/lower figure of $386.34 also appeared in search results — treat $399.47 as the more current figure from the primary source, but **reconfirm the live rate before quoting**, as it is budget-indexed.) | Contact your electricity retailer by phone, or request an application form; provide concession card and a recent bill. Applied automatically as a bill deduction once registered. Card-operated (prepay) meter customers (e.g. Ergon) have it applied to their power card automatically. |
| **Reticulated Natural Gas Rebate** | Same concession card holders as above, who use piped natural gas at their principal residence. | **$92.12/year** (figure sourced from search snippet — verify on qld.gov.au). | Apply through your gas retailer, same process as the electricity rebate. |
| **Pensioner Rate Subsidy (council rates)** | Hold a Queensland PCC (Centrelink or DVA) or DVA Gold Card (all conditions); must be owner or life tenant of the property as principal residence and legally responsible for rates. | 20% of gross rates and charges, **capped at $200/year**. Some councils add their own additional remissions on top. | Apply directly with your local council — not automatic; check for a council-specific pensioner rebate form (e.g. Brisbane City Council, Sunshine Coast Council). |
| **Seniors Card (QLD)** | QLD residents 65+ (or 60-64 meeting certain criteria) working under 35 hrs/week. | Business discounts plus the electricity rebate eligibility noted above; some public transport concessions. | Apply via qld.gov.au / Services Australia-linked process. |

**QLD verification note:** the electricity rebate and gas rebate dollar figures are both reset in the state budget — reconfirm on qld.gov.au (Cost of Living Support → Concessions) before use.

## Western Australia (WA)

| Program | Eligibility | Value | How to Apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Energy Assistance Payment (EAP)** (under the Energy Concession Extension Scheme) | Hold a PCC, HCC, CSHC, or DVA Gold Card. A **WA Seniors Card alone does not qualify.** Tenants generally need 3+ months at the residence or a lease of 3+ months. | **Conflicting figures found: $342.85/year vs $377.14/year (from 1 July 2026)** in different sources. **Flagged — must be reconfirmed directly with Synergy/Horizon Power or wa.gov.au before quoting a client**, as this is one of the least certain figures in this document. | Synergy or Horizon Power customers: automatic once concession card details are registered with the retailer — no separate application needed. Embedded network/third-party billed customers: apply to RevenueWA directly. |
| **Household Electricity Credit (2025-26 one-off)** | Automatic for essentially all WA households (Synergy/Horizon Power customers) and small businesses under 50MWh/year usage — not means-tested. | **$700 total household credit** (two instalments of $350 — one applied July–August 2025-26 billing, second December–January) — **this was a one-off cost-of-living measure; confirm whether a further round has been announced for 2026-27**, as one-off credits are not guaranteed to recur. | Automatic for Synergy/Horizon Power customers. Embedded network customers apply via RevenueWA. |
| **Dependent Child Rebate** | EAP-eligible households with dependent children. | **$396.56 per child/year** (search-sourced figure — verify). | Same process as EAP — through retailer or RevenueWA. |
| **Air Conditioning Rebate** | EAP-eligible households in specified regional/high-heat towns. | **$78.98/month** during the eligible season in specified regional towns (search-sourced — verify eligible town list and figure). | Same process as EAP. |
| **Local Government (Council) Rates Rebate — Pensioners** | Own and reside in the property as at 1 July of the rating year, hold a PCC. | Up to **50% of current year's rates**, capped at **$750 (2025-26)** — covers council rates, Emergency Services Levy, underground power charges, and water service charges. Deferral of rates is also an option. | Register via the Water Corporation's common rebate registration service (online, or phone 1300 659 951), which applies the rebate across council rates and water on the pensioner's behalf. |
| **Local Government (Council) Rates Rebate — Seniors Card** | Own and reside in the property as at 1 July of the rating year, hold a WA Seniors Card. | Up to **25% of current year's rates, capped at $100 (2025-26)** — notably lower than the pensioner rebate. | Same registration process as above (Water Corporation). |

**WA verification note:** WA has the most fragmented rebate structure of the eight jurisdictions (multiple stacked payments — EAP, Dependent Child, Air Conditioning, one-off Household Electricity Credit — each with different figures found in different sources). This state warrants the most careful re-verification of any in this matrix before advising a client, ideally by calling RevenueWA (1300 368 364) or Synergy directly.

## South Australia (SA)

| Program | Eligibility | Value | How to Apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost of Living Concession (COLC)** | SA resident who is a tenant or homeowner on a low or fixed income; typically PCC, HCC, CSHC, DVA card, or other qualifying low-income status. Since 2015, this single payment **replaced separate energy and council rate concessions** — it is a single combined cash payment, not bill-specific credits. | **$270.60 (2026-27)**, up from $261.90 (2025-26) — indexed annually. **This single payment is intended to cover council rates, energy bills, and medical costs combined** (i.e., it is not on top of separate rate/energy rebates the way other states structure it). **Flagged: the official dhs.sa.gov.au page could not be directly fetched during this research (403 error) — the figures above are from search snippets and should be confirmed directly against dhs.sa.gov.au or sa.gov.au.** | Apply via sa.gov.au or by calling the Concessions SA Hotline 1800 307 758 (TTY/speech impairment: 8226 6789). Must apply by 31 December each year for that financial year's payment. No need to reapply if circumstances are unchanged and you received it previously — paid automatically by EFT between August and December. |
| **Seniors Card (SA)** | SA residents 60+ working under 20 hrs/week. | Business discounts (hearing aids, travel, insurance, etc. referenced); no direct energy/rates cash value — SA folds cost-of-living cash support into COLC above rather than a Seniors Card-linked energy rebate. | Apply via sa.gov.au. |

**SA verification note:** because SA structures its support as one combined cash concession rather than itemised energy/rates rebates, be careful when comparing "value" across states — a client may receive less itemised detail but a broader-purpose lump sum. Reconfirm the 2026-27 amount and the 31 December application deadline directly with Concessions SA before advising a client.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Annual Electricity Concession** | Hold a PCC, HCC, or DVA Gold Card (TPI/War Widow endorsed cards also qualify). Must be the person responsible for paying the account, at their principal place of residence (not holiday homes or investment/rental properties). | Applied as a daily discount of **184.347 cents/day (2025-26 rate)** — this equates to roughly **$673/year** if applied for a full 365-day year (this annualised figure is a derived estimate, not a figure stated directly by the source, so **flag for confirmation**). | Contact your electricity retailer (e.g. Aurora Energy) directly to apply, or via Service Tasmania. ## Australian Capital Territory (ACT)

| Program | Eligibility | Value | How to Apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Electricity, Gas and Water Rebate** (formerly "Utilities Concession") | Primary holder of a PCC, Low Income HCC, HCC, DVA Veteran Gold Card (specific endorsements — POW, War Widow, or TPI), or ACT Services Access Card. | **$800/year for 2026-27** (confirmed consistently across two independent sources). | **Rates deferral (65+)** | ACT homeowners 65+ with at least 75% equity in their home. | Indefinite deferral of rates until the property is sold/transferred, with concessional interest accruing on the deferred amount. | Apply via ACT Revenue Office. |
| **Seniors Card (ACT)** | ACT residents 60+ working under 20 hrs/week. | 10% off motor vehicle registration; 28% discount on EV registration; discounted entry to tourist attractions and other local/national business deals. | Apply via Access Canberra / act.gov.au. |

**ACT verification note:** this jurisdiction's figures were the most consistently corroborated across independent sources in this research round ($800 rebate, $750 rates cap, $133 levy rebate) — still reconfirm annually since ACT typically resets these each budget.

## Northern Territory (NT)

| Program | Eligibility | Value | How to Apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NT Concession Scheme — Electricity** | Must be a member of the NT Concession Scheme: live in the NT 183+ days/year, be an Australian citizen or permanent resident, and receive an eligible Centrelink/DVA payment (Age Pension, Disability Support Pension, Carer Payment, Parenting Payment single, and some DVA cards). Only one member per household can claim. | Up to **$1,200/year** (approx. 8,000kWh), calculated as a fixed daily rate (~$1.158/day) plus a per-kWh credit (~$0.082/kWh) for Jacana Energy customers — Power and Water Corporation customers have an equivalent structure. **Flag: reconfirm the exact rate structure and cap, as this is the highest headline energy figure of any state and should be double-checked before quoting.** | Provide your NT Concession Scheme membership number to Jacana Energy or Power and Water Corporation; they apply the concession directly. |
| **NT Concession Scheme — Council Rates & Garbage** | Same NT Concession Scheme membership as above. | Up to **$200/year** for council rates and up to **$150/year** for garbage collection (varies by council/region). | Provide concession membership details to your local council. |
| **NT Concession Scheme — other benefits** | Same membership. | Also covers water/sewerage, vehicle registration, and driver's licence concessions. Combined, sources suggest total potential value of **~$2,990+/year** across all bundled concessions for a fully eligible household — treat this aggregate figure as indicative only, not a guaranteed amount for any individual client. | Apply to become an NT Concession Scheme member via nt.gov.au (email ntconcessionandrecognition@nt.gov.au or visit a Territory Business Centre / customer service centre) — once a member, individual concessions listed above generally don't need separate re-applications. |
| **NT Seniors Recognition Scheme** | NT Concession Scheme members aged 65+. | Additional discounts (e.g. free bus travel in Darwin/Alice Springs, ~$55 vehicle registration concession per search sources) on top of the base concession scheme. | Apply/verify via the same NT Concession Scheme membership process. |

**NT verification note:** the NT bundles nearly everything (energy, rates, garbage, water, rego, licence) under one membership, which is unique among the eight jurisdictions — good news for a navigation service, since one successful application likely unlocks several concessions at once. Reconfirm the $1,200 electricity cap and the aggregate ~$2,990 figure directly with nt.gov.au, as both were sourced from search snippets/aggregators rather than a directly fetched primary page.

## Cross-state observations (for service design)

- **Card eligibility is not uniform.** Most states require a PCC, HCC, or DVA Gold Card; the CSHC (held by self-funded retirees on the Age Pension-equivalent age but over the income test) is explicitly **excluded** from most energy rebates (NSW, QLD, WA all confirmed this exclusion) — a common source of client confusion worth flagging early in any client conversation.
- **Automatic vs. apply-yourself varies by program, not just by state.** Energy rebates are usually applied automatically once a concession card is registered with the retailer (QLD, VIC, ACT, WA-EAP, NT). Council rate rebates are almost always **not automatic** and require a direct application to the local council (NSW, VIC, QLD, TAS, WA) — this is one of the most common reasons eligible people miss out, and a strong potential value-add for a navigation service.
- **QLD and NT are unusual** in extending energy rebate eligibility to Seniors Card holders alone (not just pension-tested cards) — worth checking specifically for older clients who are not pensioners but do hold a Seniors Card.
- **SA is structurally different** — it pays one combined Cost of Living Concession rather than separate itemised energy/rates rebates, which simplifies applying but makes "value per category" harder to state precisely.
- **WA is the most complex/fragmented** with multiple stacked payments and the largest number of unverified/conflicting figures found in this research — prioritise re-verification here before relying on any dollar figure in client-facing material.
- The **federal universal energy credit has ended (31 Dec 2025)** — clients may still be expecting the automatic quarterly bill credits from 2025 and should be told these are gone; only the state concessions above remain as an ongoing baseline as of the research date.
