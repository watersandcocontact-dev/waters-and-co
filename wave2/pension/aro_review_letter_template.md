# DRAFT TEMPLATE — Request for Authorised Review Officer (ARO) Review

**Research date:** 29 July 2026
**Status: DRAFT / TEMPLATE ONLY.** This document is a preparation aid for the client. It is **not legal or financial advice**, and it is **not a lodged request**. The client must submit the request themselves — via their own myGov-authenticated Centrelink online account, the official **SS351 "Explanation or formal review of a decision"** form, by phone, or in person at a service centre. The entrepreneur preparing this template does **not** submit it to Centrelink on the client's behalf.

---

## IMPORTANT — Get the process right (read before using this template)

### The review pathway
1. **(Optional) Ask for an explanation.** A client can call Centrelink and ask an independent staff member to explain how a decision was made. This step is **not compulsory** — a client who already understands the decision but disagrees with it can go straight to step 2.
2. **Request a formal review by an Authorised Review Officer (ARO).** The ARO is an independent Centrelink officer (not the original decision-maker) who re-examines the decision. This is free, and can be requested by phone, in writing (form **SS351**), or through the client's Centrelink online account.
3. **If still unhappy after the ARO decision — apply to the Administrative Review Tribunal (ART)** for an independent external review. The ART is a Commonwealth tribunal, external to Centrelink/Services Australia. **Note: the ART replaced the former Administrative Appeals Tribunal (AAT) — the AAT was abolished and the ART commenced operation on 14 October 2024.** For social security matters, a two-tier structure has been retained within the ART (a first review, and in some cases a further "second review" within the Tribunal) — carried over from the old AAT structure. Confirm the exact current tier names and process on [art.gov.au](https://www.art.gov.au/applying-review/centrelink) at time of use, as tribunal branding/procedure can change.

### On the "13-week" rule — READ CAREFULLY, do not misstate this to clients
This is commonly misunderstood, so get it precisely right:

- **It is NOT a hard deadline that bars a client from requesting an ARO review after 13 weeks.** A client CAN still ask for a formal ARO review after 13 weeks have passed.
- **What the 13 weeks actually controls is backdating/arrears.** Services Australia's guidance is: you should apply for a formal review of most claim and entitlement decisions **within 13 weeks from the date the decision was communicated to you**. If you do this, and the ARO changes the decision in your favour, the change can be backdated to the original decision date. **If you apply after the 13-week window, and the decision is changed in your favour, the change generally only takes effect from the date you applied for the review** — not backdated to the original decision. In other words: late review requests are still accepted, but a client who delays risks losing back-payment/arrears for the period between the original decision and when they actually lodged the review request.
- **A separate, different 13-week figure exists elsewhere in the Age Pension system** — the window in which a person can *lodge a new pension claim* before reaching Age Pension age. That is a claim-timing rule, unrelated to the review/backdating rule above. Do not conflate the two when advising clients.
- **There is also a distinct time limit at the next stage (ART application)** if the client escalates beyond the ARO. Depending on the payment type, ART application time limits vary (e.g. some payment categories have a defined number of days to apply once the ARO decision is received, and for most Centrelink decisions there may be no strict lodging deadline — but again, applying late can limit how far back a favourable ART decision is backdated, commonly also referenced against a 13-week backdating benchmark). **This detail should be re-verified directly against art.gov.au and the client's specific decision letter at the time of use**, because the exact limit differs by payment/decision type and the decision letter itself should state the applicable limit.

**Bottom line for client advice:** encourage the client to request a review **as soon as possible after receiving a decision they disagree with — ideally within 13 weeks** — to preserve full backdating of any favourable outcome, while being clear that a review can still be requested later if needed.

## What information the client needs to gather before requesting an ARO review

- [ ] Full name and Centrelink Customer Reference Number (CRN)
- [ ] The **decision being disputed** — payment type (e.g. Age Pension), and precisely what was decided (e.g. rate reduction, rejection, assets test outcome, start date, overpayment/debt raised)
- [ ] **Date the decision was made / date it was communicated** to the client (check the decision letter — this date starts the 13-week backdating clock)
- [ ] Reference/letter number from the decision notice, if shown
- [ ] A clear, specific statement of **why the client disagrees** with the decision (facts, calculation, or law they believe is wrong)
- [ ] **Supporting evidence** for their position — e.g. corrected bank statements, valuations, payslips, medical evidence, proof of a date or event Centrelink had wrong
- [ ] Contact details (phone/email) — the ARO will generally try to contact the client to discuss the review before deciding
- [ ] Whether the client has already asked for an "explanation" of the decision (not required, but useful context)

## DRAFT LETTER TEMPLATE

*(For the client's own use/submission — via their myGov/Centrelink online account, the SS351 form, in writing, or by phone request read out to a Centrelink officer. This is not a document the entrepreneur transmits to Centrelink.)*

```
[Client's full name]
Centrelink Customer Reference Number (CRN): [__________]
[Client's address]
[Client's phone number / email]

Date: [__________]

To: Services Australia / Centrelink — Authorised Review Officer

RE: Request for formal review of Centrelink decision — [payment type, e.g. Age Pension]

Decision reference / letter date: [decision reference number, if any] / [date decision was made or communicated]

I am writing to request a formal review by an Authorised Review Officer of the
following Centrelink decision:

Decision disputed:
[Describe the decision precisely — e.g. "the decision dated [DD/MM/YYYY] to
reduce my Age Pension rate due to an assessment of my assets" or "the decision
to reject my Age Pension claim lodged on [DD/MM/YYYY]"]

Reasons I disagree with this decision:
1. [Reason 1 — plain, specific statement of fact/error]
2. [Reason 2, if applicable]
3. [Reason 3, if applicable]

Supporting evidence attached / referenced:
- [Document 1 — e.g. bank statement showing correct balance as at [date]]
- [Document 2 — e.g. property valuation report dated [date]]
- [Document 3 — e.g. superannuation statement]
- [Document 4 — other]

I understand I can be contacted to discuss this review. My preferred contact
method is: [phone / online account message / email]

I am requesting this review within the timeframe that preserves backdating of
any corrected entitlement (or, if outside 13 weeks, I understand any change may
only apply from the date of this request, and I am requesting the review
regardless).

Signed: _______________________
[Client's full name]
Date: __________
```

## Reminder for the entrepreneur — scope of assistance

- This template helps the client organise their reasons and evidence clearly.
- The entrepreneur may help the client **draft and structure** this content.
- The **client must submit it themselves** — logging into their own myGov/Centrelink online account, mailing/lodging the SS351 form under their own signature, or phoning Centrelink directly and quoting their own CRN — because Centrelink requires the account holder's own authentication for actions on their record.
- The entrepreneur should **not** ask for or hold the client's myGov password, and should **not** submit this request to Centrelink on the client's behalf.
- If the ARO review is unsuccessful, the next step (external review at the Administrative Review Tribunal) is a separate, more formal legal process — the entrepreneur's service is claim/review **preparation and document support**, not legal representation, and clients with complex ART matters should be encouraged to also seek free advice from a community legal centre / welfare rights service (e.g. Economic Justice Australia member services) if needed.

Re-check the following before relying on this template with real clients, since these are the parts most likely to have changed or to need a precise re-read of the live page/decision letter:

1. **Exact current wording of the 13-week backdating rule** on the live Services Australia page — confirm it still reads as "you should apply within 13 weeks… you can still apply after 13 weeks… backdating may be limited to the date you applied" rather than having become a hard deadline.
2. **ART application time limits** after an ARO decision — these differ by payment/decision type (this research found references to no fixed deadline for many Centrelink/social security decisions but a 13-week backdating-equivalent principle, versus fixed day-count deadlines for some other payment types such as Paid Parental Leave and family assistance) — the client's own decision letter should state the applicable limit, and this should be checked against art.gov.au at time of use.
3. **Current name, tiers and branding of the Administrative Review Tribunal** — confirm "first review" / "second review" terminology (and whether informal shorthand like "ART1"/"ART2" is still used) is current, as tribunal naming and structure could be refined post-transition from the AAT.
